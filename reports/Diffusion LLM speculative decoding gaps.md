# Block drafters still lack fresh target computation

As of November 2026, diffusion-style drafting is the default way to speculate for AR targets. DFlash (ICML 2026) has about 20 published follow-ups, and almost all of them do one of four things. They rerank or correct the same per-position marginals (LiLiCorr, DCOCO, Domino, DARTree, TreeFlash). They build trees over those marginals (DDTree, BASTION, CaDDTree, PRESTO, DominoTree). They reuse the previous round's verifier states over the rejected suffix (DFlow, Carryover, ReTrace). Or they tune training and serving (Teaching L2R, DScout, Draft-OPD, DScale, H-Spec). Typical gains over DFlash are **+5 to +20% tau**, with a step change only when trees are added. No paper found gives a block drafter fresh target-like computation over the tokens of the *current* draft before verification, whether from a cheap copy, an intermediate verifier or an in-network pass. Your measurements say this is exactly where the remaining acceptance sits: every copy-free route caps near **10.4 accepted**, and redrafting from a copy's features reaches about **14**. The other lines rest on different assumptions and are unlikely to give you the missing information. Speculation for dLLM targets defines "lossless" against the dLLM's own slower decoder. Lossless verification rules have no derivation for trees built from conditionally independent marginals, and their gains vanish at T=0. Hybrid AR-diffusion models get target-quality computation over drafted tokens only by retraining the backbone. Reasoning traces are the domain where block drafters lose most, and no block-drafter paper explains why. Ranked for a single-mechanism MSc thesis on DFlash with Qwen3-8B at batch 1, the strongest open gaps are these. First, who performs within-round target computation over the draft, if it is not a bolt-on copy. Second, why block drafters collapse on thinking traces. Third, lossless verification of stochastic trees drawn from product-of-marginals proposals. The first has the largest measured ceiling and the most scoop risk. The second is the most clearly open. The third is the cleanest single mechanism but has the smallest ceiling.

## Five lines of work, each with its own definition of the problem

All numbers are as the papers report them, with model and setting. "Verification" in the right-hand column records how much of each paper was actually read: full text, abstract only, or an aggregator or secondary summary.

### Block-diffusion and dLLM drafters for AR targets reuse old target states, never fresh ones

DFlash set the template. It uses 5 draft layers and block 16, and it injects hidden states from 5 target layers into the K/V of every draft layer. On Qwen3-8B with thinking off it reports **4.86x and tau 6.49 at T=0, and 4.03x and tau 5.48 at T=1** ([DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v2)). Every follow-up keeps its core assumption: drafted positions see target features only up to the anchor. Since June 2026 the dominant way to add target information has been to recycle the last verification's states over the rejected suffix. These states come at no extra cost, but they are conditioned on a wrong token and are one round stale. Coherence-correction papers stay inside the same marginals, and LiLiCorr states that its supervision exists only when the ground truth is in the top-K.

Two items here bear directly on your work. First, the quantized-target competition paper trains a block drafter on the FP target and then adapts it to a *quantized verifier*. ### Speculation for dLLM targets verifies against the dLLM's own slower sampler

At least nine draft-and-verify methods target the dLLM itself, and nearly all are self-speculative. They use "lossless" in three different senses. In one, the output is identical to stepwise or one-token-per-step greedy decoding (SSD, FreeDave). In another, the next denoising-state transition is preserved (Spiffy, SPOT). In the third, the method is exact for a non-factorized joint that it defines itself (SSMD, FeF-DLLM). None verifies against the trained bidirectional model's joint, because that joint is not tractable. Training-free self-speculation clusters at **2-3x**. Headlines of 7-14x come from stacking on threshold parallel decoding or approximate caches.

The structural lesson transfers in one direction only. SPOT finds that the dLLM's previous pass already holds the right token at almost every position, so its bottleneck is order. That premise fails for an AR target with a block drafter. You measured that alternatives rescue only 8-25% of DFlash's forks, so token content is what is missing. ParallelBench formalizes the shared object, the error from replacing a joint by a product of marginals. Its conditional total-correlation bound shows the error is unavoidable even for an ideal model, and it reports that adaptive thresholds sit far below a per-sample oracle ([ParallelBench note](https://en.papernotes.org/ICLR2026/llm_evaluation/parallelbench_understanding_the_trade-offs_of_parallel_decoding_in_diffusion_llm/)). Revocable decoding (WINO, LLaDA2.1, Token-to-Mask, DARD) improves accuracy, but no paper read measures whether parallel-decoding errors are local or propagate. Token-to-Mask reports that only **1.2% of remasked positions refill with a different token** ([T2M](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18738)).

### Lossless verification theory stops where conditionally independent proposals begin

Lossless rules are mature for three settings: single-step multi-draft (OT optimum, canonical decomposition, Global Resolution), single chains (Block Verification, HSD) and AR-drafted trees (Traversal Verification, UniVer, Layer Verification). Every one assumes drafts sampled from an autoregressive draft model, either i.i.d. or by a top-(m-1)+1 mixed strategy. The block-diffusion tree papers build deterministic top-B prefix trees and verify by walking the tree with the target's own decoding rule. DDTree proves its tree optimal only for the factorized surrogate, and it states that sampling-mode losslessness of the walk is not analyzed separately ([DDTree](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12989)).

### Hybrid models buy in-network verification by retraining the target

TiDAR and Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion draft and verify inside one network, and both lose a little quality against the original checkpoint. Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion trains on 1T AR plus 300B joint tokens from Ministral3. Its 8B instruct model reaches **5.99x tokens per forward with LoRA linear self-speculation** and an average of 62.81 against 63.61 for AR, all in non-thinking mode. It lists "weak long-range parallelism" and prefix-only verification as limitations ([Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05722v1)). Two lines keep a frozen next-token path. Apple's gated-LoRA mask tokens report average accepted lengths of 1.67 / 2.69 / 3.17 for 1 / 4 / 8 masks on Tulu3-8B after about 1M examples and 50k iterations, with no per-offset accuracy ([Apple](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11851v1)). Uno/UnoQwen adds rank-128 gated LoRA "diffusion weights" to every layer of a frozen Qwen3-8B and reports **acceptance 3.89 against 2.07 for DFlash at T=1** ([Uno](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04010)). FastMTP reaches chained per-step acceptance of about 80% / 56% / 36% on frozen MiMo-7B with 389K self-distilled samples ([FastMTP](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18362v1)). A PEFT negative result on Qwen3-0.6B finds that a same-backbone LoRA diffusion drafter reaches 34 tok/s against 188 for FastMTP, because drafting and verification are both full passes ([2607.12422](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12422)). Your mask-slot heads (0.68 / 0.31 top-1 at offsets 1 / 4 after 8000 sequences) were trained on roughly two orders of magnitude less data than these references. They are therefore not evidence against the approach at scale.

### Reasoning traces are where block drafters lose most, and nobody explains why

DFlash's own thinking-mode drafters on Qwen3-8B at T=0 reach **tau 5.17 (GPQA), 5.82 (MATH-500) and 5.74 (AIME25)**, against 6.49 averaged without thinking. The paper gives no explanation and separately reports degradation beyond 4K context ([DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v2)). Your own measurement is sharper: tau 3.65 in thinking mode against 7.83 on normal answers. Under a flat-hazard model, (1-p^16)/(1-p) = 3.65 means a per-token hazard of roughly 27%, against the ~10% you measure on normal text. That is my arithmetic, not a paper result. The only diagnostic of trace structure uses AR drafters. On R1-Distill-Qwen-7B across 1.78M verification rounds, acceptance does not decay with position once a greedy repetition pathology is removed. Rejections concentrate on reflection triggers ("wait", "however", "check"), and LoRA alignment aimed at those tokens did not raise mean accepted length ([Meng 2610.30644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30644), abstract only, single author, not peer reviewed). DCOCO is the only 2026 block-drafter paper found that evaluates thinking on and off ([alphaXiv](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.12570)). D2SD, D-Loop, ReTrace, DSpark, TiDAR and Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion report no thinking-mode results.

## Ten assumptions almost every paper shares

The first and most consequential assumption is that **drafted positions see target computation only up to the anchor, or one round stale**. DFlash, every coherence-correction method and every tree builder condition on anchor features only. DFlow, Carryover and ReTrace add previous-round states over tokens that were already rejected. The hybrid models are the exception, and they pay for it with a retrained backbone.

Second, **the per-position marginals are treated as independent**. Tree builders use this explicitly as a surrogate (DDTree, BASTION), while correction heads assume the right token is already in a top-K shortlist. LiLiCorr states that the top-K cap limits its supervision. CaDDTree states that independence "can accelerate error accumulation" ([CaDDTree](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01813)).

Third, **acceptance is prefix-only**, so nothing after the first error matters. Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion names this as a limitation, because confident non-prefix tokens are discarded. Your finding that DFlash does not heal after its first error makes the assumption costless for DFlash as built. It also means that any non-contiguous acceptance scheme would first need a drafter that heals.

Fourth, **trees are deterministic top-B sets**, which makes the drafter's probabilities one-hot as far as probabilistic verifiers can see. HSD says its rule "degenerates" under EAGLE-3 top-K drafting ([HSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05724v2)).

Fifth, **headlines come from batch 1, T=0, short context and thinking off**. Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion is explicitly non-thinking. BASTION, PRESTO and TiDAR are batch 1 only, and the concurrency sweeps that do exist show gains shrinking or reversing (DominoTree's lead over the chain reverses at 8 or more requests) ([DominoTree](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08642)).

Sixth, **verification cost is treated as flat in tree width** at batch 1. This holds in your measurements for the verifier (13.3 ms at 16 nodes, 15.8 ms at 128). It does not hold for a cheap copy, which grows from 3 ms to 10.5 ms. Every hierarchy paper read (HiSpec, Polybasic) is chain-only, so none confronts this asymmetry.

Seventh, **one drafter per target, all reading the same features**. Every alternative-generation method found draws its alternatives from one DFlash pass. D2SD's "error homogeneity" result (resampling from the same marginals adds +0.37 tau) is the only published hint of what your error-correlation measurements show directly.

Eighth, **"lossless" is asserted and not audited numerically**. On the dLLM side the reference is the dLLM's own slower decoder. On the AR side, a BF16 audit finds only 43-45% greedy match ([How Lossless Is Lossless SD?](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15504v1)). Neither the papers nor your 5.8 (vLLM online) against 8.0 (offline) tau gap has a published explanation.

Ninth, **evaluation is mostly the Qwen3 family**. DFlow and Carryover use Qwen3 (Carryover adds Gemma 4 12B), DCOCO uses one B200/vLLM setup and says it is "not a claim of universal speedup", and TreeFlash states that it is Qwen-only.

Tenth, **wall clock is often replaced by tau or NFE**. Spiffy, Apple MTP and DBLAST report acceptance or NFE, and the dLLM papers that report wall clock show much smaller gains than their NFE headlines.

## Seven open gaps, ranked for one novel mechanism

The ranking weighs four things: how clearly the gap is open in the literature, how large your own measurements say the ceiling is, whether a single mechanism (rather than a combination of existing parts) can fill it, and how exposed it is to a scoop. Compute cost is not used as a criterion.

### Gap 1: who computes target-like states over the current draft, if not a bolt-on copy

**Evidence that it is open.** No paper found uses a cheap, quantized, pruned or early-exit version of the target to compute features over a block drafter's current draft, and no paper inserts an intermediate verifier between a block drafter and an AR target. The nearest work stops one step short in each direction. DFlow, Carryover and ReTrace use target states that are free but stale, conditioned on the wrong token. The quantized-target paper quantizes the verifier itself. HiSpec and Polybasic place an intermediate verifier only between AR drafters and targets, on chains, and HiSpec lists trees as future work. Carryover's authors write that "stronger drafters appear to leave more useful rejected states" and leave multi-round and tree retention open ([Carryover](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14717v1)).

**Relation to your measurements.** This gap carries the largest ceiling you have measured. Real verifier features at in-flight positions give 13.81 against 8.50 tokens per verification. A 4-bit copy reaches the real-feature level (12.53 vs 12.54), and redrafting from copy features reaches about 14, while every copy-free route sits near 10.4. Your negative results already rule out the cheap substitutes: learned emulators, placeholders, maps from drafter states and untrained pruning. They point to the principle that the information exists only in target-like computation over the drafted tokens. The constraint is that your supervisor has said a plain quantized copy refreshing features is not publishable on its own, and a "copy + DFlash + redraft" pipeline is the kind of combination you want to avoid. The novelty therefore has to sit in the mechanism that produces or uses the computation. One option is an intermediate stage trained for the block drafter's needs rather than for fidelity to the target; your exp81, which builds an estimator from the target's own tap layers, sits here. Another is an acceptance rule that makes such a stage a lossless verifier of forks. Both framings are my inference, not proposals from any paper.

**Closest prior work that could overturn a novelty claim.** HiSpec, Polybasic SD, ML-SpecQD and "SD meets quantization" cover intermediate verifiers, but for AR drafters. DFlow, Carryover and ReTrace cover verifier-state reuse. Uno/UnoQwen and TiDAR/Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion put the computation inside the target. Packed-SS (2610.33379), which drafts one call ahead in the same model call, should also be checked. From your literature sheet, FlexDraft (2605.20022), ReAnchor and DLoop need a re-check for any newer version that adds copy features. OpenReview ICLR 2027 submissions were not searched and are the main scoop risk.

**Decisive first experiment.** Port the copy-refresh redraft to vLLM at batch 1 on Qwen3-8B and measure online tokens per verification and tokens/s. Compare it against DFlash, DDTree-64 and a DFlow-style rejected-state reuse reimplemented on the same drafter. Your offline-to-online tau drop (8.0 to 5.8) is the threat. If the ~14 vs ~10.4 advantage shrinks in proportion, the line survives. If it collapses online, the information gain is partly an offline artifact, and the mechanism question becomes moot.

### Gap 2: why block drafters collapse on thinking traces

**Evidence that it is open.** DFlash reports the drop in thinking mode without explaining it. Meng's diagnostic covers AR drafters only and finds rejections concentrated at reflection triggers with no positional decay. DCOCO evaluates thinking mode, but only as a benchmark condition. No paper measures per-position hazard for a block drafter on reasoning traces. None separates trace type from context length, either, although DFlash reports degradation beyond 4K and thinking traces are long.

**Relation to your measurements.** Your hazard on normal text is flat at ~10% and does not heal. Your thinking-mode tau of 3.65 implies roughly 27% under the same model, so either the hazard rises everywhere or it spikes at a small set of fork tokens. These two cases lead to different mechanisms. A spike that the copy's margin can flag (your AUROC of 0.961 for copy disagreements) suggests fork-targeted computation. A uniform rise suggests a distribution or capacity problem that no inference mechanism will fix. This gap also matters most for deployment, because reasoning models are where decode time goes.

**Closest prior work.** Meng (2610.30644) for the trigger hypothesis. D2SD and D-Loop for re-anchoring at likely failures. DFlash's own long-context fine-tune. Lookahead Reasoning and SpecReason, which are step-level and lossy. Teaching L2R, which reports gains on reasoning benchmarks through training alone.

**Decisive first experiment.** On Qwen3-8B with DFlash's thinking drafter at T=0, log per-position hazard at a correct prefix on thinking traces. Stratify it by context-length bin, by token class (reflection triggers vs other) and by target entropy, and compare against non-thinking traces truncated to matched context lengths. The decision rule: if a small share of positions (say under 5%) accounts for a large share of failures (say over 40%), a fork-targeted mechanism has a ceiling worth a paper. If the hazard is flat and simply higher, this gap becomes a training-data problem.

### Gap 3: lossless verification of stochastic trees sampled from product-of-marginals proposals

**Evidence that it is open.** The notes found no paper that derives a lossless or optimal acceptance rule for trees whose children are sampled from conditionally independent per-position marginals. Global Resolution lists non-i.i.d. drafts and multi-step evaluation as future work. Hu et al. leave multi-step trees open, and UniVer leaves co-design of sampling, verification and tree open. DDTree builds the same tree at T=0 and T=1, and the paper map found that its T=1 DFlash baseline drafts greedily.

**Relation to your measurements.** All your core numbers are at T=0, where UniVer and Traversal report no gain, so this gap has zero ceiling in your current setup. Its value appears only at T>0 and in deployment, where your online tau is already lower. It is the cleanest fit for "one novel mechanism", because a verification rule stands alone. Your own Tree-HSD (reach-reweighted recursive rejection over draft trees) sits here.

**Closest prior work.** Traversal Verification's residual norm([p·Mb - Ms]+) already weights the parent's mass by path acceptance. UniVer uses "prefix acceptance probabilities as scaling factors". Layer Verification balances acceptance per layer (abstract only). HSD, DBLAST (training for T>0) and SPOT's coupled-noise verification for dLLM targets are also close. Gains reported on real LLMs are 2.2-8.5% over RRSw, so the ceiling is modest.

**Decisive first experiment.** At T=1 on Qwen3-8B, compare three things on the same DFlash marginals: DDTree's deterministic top-B walk, children sampled without replacement and verified by Traversal or UniVer, and the exact LP optimum computed for small trees (B up to 16). If the optimum beats the deterministic walk by less than about 5% tau, the gap is real but too small for a thesis.

### Gap 4: target states from the non-accepted parts of a wide verify tree

**Evidence that it is open.** DDTree, BASTION, PRESTO and DARTree discard the target's states over non-accepted tree nodes. DFlow and Carryover reuse rejected states only along a chain, and Carryover names "which rejected branch states to keep" in tree verification as open.

**Relation to your measurements.** At batch 1, verifier width is nearly free (13.3 to 15.8 ms from 16 to 128 nodes), so a wide tree computes real target states over many drafted tokens at no extra cost. Your data also says the most useful states are those conditioned on a correct prefix. Only the branch matching the target's bonus token provides those, and only for a few positions past the fork. The ceiling is therefore probably small: below the copy route, and possibly close to DFlow's +10-15%.

**Closest prior work.** Carryover (which names this as its own future work, so the scoop risk is high), DFlow, ReTrace, DPara (2609.27396, which precomputes drafts for every acceptance boundary) and Speculative Speculative Decoding (2603.03251).

**Decisive first experiment.** Offline, during DDTree-64 verification, keep the target states for all nodes. Measure next-round tau under three conditions: DDTree alone, the rejected chain's states (Carryover-style), and the states of the subtree under the bonus token. If the third beats the second by under ~0.3 tau, drop the gap.

### Gap 5: in-target mask-slot lookahead on a frozen target, now largely closed

**Evidence.** Uno/UnoQwen already adds gated diffusion weights to every layer of a frozen Qwen3-8B with lossless verification. Apple and FastMTP show frozen-path heads scaling with data, and TiDAR and Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion cover the retrained case. What remains open is a measurement, not a method: no paper reports per-offset top-1 at offsets 1, 4 and 8 together with a data-scaling curve for mask-slot heads.

**Relation to your measurements.** Your heads are at 0.68 / 0.31 against DFlash's 0.82 / 0.59 after 8000 sequences, which is the data-limited regime by any of these references.

**Decisive experiment.** Train at 8K, 32K and 128K sequences and fit the offset-4 curve. If the curve does not project past 0.59, the in-target route is dominated by DFlash. If it does, the method is still Uno's.

### Gap 6: drafter diversity chosen for error decorrelation

**Evidence that it is open.** No paper measures error correlation between drafters or designs for it. SpecDiff-2 lists K specialized drafters as future work, and the Findings-ACL 2026 multi-drafter paper was not read.

**Relation to your measurements.** You have the only measurement found anywhere: drafters reading the same target features fail at the same positions, and a text-only drafter (PARD) is right at ~45% of DFlash's forks. But every copy-free route you tested caps near DFlash's second choice (~0.56 fork accuracy). That makes the ceiling low unless PARD's correct forks fall mostly where DFlash's second choice is wrong.

**Decisive experiment.** At DFlash fork positions, compute oracle fork accuracy for the union of DFlash's top-2 and PARD's top-1. If the union stays below ~0.65, the gap cannot approach the copy route and should be dropped.

### Gap 7: a full dLLM as repairer of an AR target's drafts, open in print but closed by your data

**Evidence.** The draft-then-refine papers found all target dLLMs. DEER and SpecDiff-2 use dLLMs as drafters only.

**Relation to your measurements.** Your bidirectional repair with Dream-7B got worse when given the draft's right context (0.42 against 0.565 fork accuracy). Together with Token-to-Mask's report that refills rarely change tokens, this argues against the mechanism. This is a useful negative result inside a larger paper, not a thesis.

Two further gaps sit outside your scope. On the dLLM-target side, no method verifies a dLLM with a separate small diffusion drafter; Spiffy lists this as future work. No paper explains the online-versus-offline tau gap, which is a measurement problem rather than a mechanism.

## Conclusion

The field has spent 2026 squeezing the information a block drafter already has. Reranking, correction heads, trees, second passes and stale rejected-state reuse all land between +5% and +20%, and your caps near 10.4 tokens and 0.56 fork accuracy show why: the information missing at a fork is not in those marginals. The two places that do have it are a fresh target-like pass over the current draft and the target itself in a retrained hybrid. The literature has filled the second (TiDAR, Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion, Uno). The first has no occupant for block drafters, which makes it the most valuable open gap. The difficulty is that its simplest filler, a quantized copy, has already been ruled out as a contribution, so a publishable method there has to change who computes or how the computation is used, not just add it.

The ranking also shows a sequencing logic. The thinking-trace diagnostic (Gap 2) is cheap to run and decides whether the copy route's ceiling grows or shrinks in the regime that matters most. If failures on reasoning traces concentrate at flaggable forks, then fork-targeted fresh computation gains its strongest motivation exactly where block drafters are weakest. The online vLLM replication (Gap 1's first experiment) decides whether the offline ceiling is real. Running both before committing to a mechanism avoids building a method on an offline artifact or on a domain where it does not help.
