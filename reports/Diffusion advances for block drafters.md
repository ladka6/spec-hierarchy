# Distill the refresh loop into the drafter

The diffusion advances most likely to help a DFlash-style block drafter are the ones that give the drafter the target's computation over its own drafted tokens at training time, so it does not have to pay for it at inference time. Three 2025-2026 lines point that way. Privileged-information and trajectory distillation (pi-Distill, FPD, SDTT, Draft-OPD) can collapse the draft -> estimate -> redraft loop into one pass. Representation alignment (REPA, REPA-E, REG) can make drafter states predict target states over drafted positions at zero inference cost. Conditioning augmentation (cascaded diffusion, Imagen) lets one drafter take feature sources of mixed reliability. The remasking and editing work from discrete diffusion (PRISM, RemeDi, LLaDA2.1, learned unmasking policies) is the right tool for a narrower problem: deciding which positions to redraft and filtering the estimator's ~20% wrong corrections. On its own, though, it mostly works in the token-only regime, which our data caps at about 36% recovery against about 66% for true target context. Caching and forecasting work (TaylorSeer, SpeCa, dLLM-Cache, Elastic-Cache) does not improve the drafter. It cuts the cost of the estimator pass, which matters because every refinement round currently costs one 0.6B pass. Two papers compete directly and must be benchmarked on our exact setup. Uno/UnoQwen (2609.04010) puts the drafter inside the target and so removes the context gap by construction. Draft-OPD (2605.29343) already does on-policy training of DFlash drafters and gains 13% over DFlash. No paper we found iterates draft -> estimate target states -> redraft before verification, and none applies REPA-style alignment to text or speculative drafters. Those two gaps are where novelty is safest.

## Few-step distillation can absorb the refinement loop

The 2025-2026 few-step literature converges on one recipe: train on the student's own rollouts and add a mode-seeking distribution-matching term on top of a consistency target. In continuous diffusion, sCM reaches 2-step ImageNet-512 FID 1.88 against a diffusion FID of 1.73 ([arXiv:2410.11081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11081)). rCM adds a DMD-style score regularizer to continuous-time consistency and scales to 14B text-to-video models at 1-4 steps, with **15-50x acceleration**, quality that matches or beats DMD2, and better diversity ([arXiv:2510.08431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08431)). Two-time models learn the jump between any two noise levels. **MeanFlow reaches 1-NFE ImageNet-256 FID 3.43** ([arXiv:2505.13447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13447)), and IMM reaches FID 1.99 in 8 steps from scratch ([arXiv:2503.07565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07565)). Shortcut models condition on step size and enforce "one 2d step equals two d steps" ([arXiv:2410.12557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12557)). Self Forcing trains autoregressive video diffusion on its own KV-cached rollouts to remove exposure bias ([arXiv:2506.08009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08009)).

Discrete diffusion has caught up. Di[M]O distills a masked image model into one step with token-level distribution matching on intermediate states built from the student's own outputs, reaching **one-step ImageNet FID 6.91 against 6.60 for the 16-step teacher** ([arXiv:2503.15457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15457)). Fixed-Point Distillation (FPD) is the closest structural match to our loop. The student drafts everything in one pass, the draft is partially re-masked and refined by one frozen teacher step, and the student regresses onto that refinement. It reaches one-step GenEval 0.45 against 0.48 for the 16-step teacher, and its key ablation shows that **refining random drafts instead of the student's own drafts drops GenEval to 0.22** ([arXiv:2605.21484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21484)). In text, SDTT iteratively halves steps with reverse KL to the teacher's 2-step output ([arXiv:2410.21035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21035)). d3LLM's pseudo-trajectory distillation lifts LLaDA to **9.11 tokens per forward on GSM8K-CoT at 73.1% accuracy** against 72.6% at 1 token per forward ([arXiv:2601.07568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07568)). T3D trains on teacher rollouts with a reverse-KL-like objective and argues that trajectory distillation lowers the factorization error of tokens decoded in parallel ([arXiv:2602.12262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12262)), which is exactly the failure of a 16-token parallel drafter.

Privileged-information distillation shows the teacher does not need to be a different model. pi-Distill trains one shared-parameter network as both a privileged-information teacher and an unprivileged student, and reaches **44.1% on TravelPlanner with Qwen3-8B against 32.3% for SFT+RL**. Its analysis finds that low teacher-student KL and useful privileged information drive success ([arXiv:2602.04942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04942)). Draft-OPD shows the on-policy part already works for DFlash: forward KL on accepted tokens and reverse KL on rejected tokens, with position weight 0.8^(k-1), raise acceptance length from 5.35 to 5.85 and reach a 4.88x average speedup on Qwen3 ([arXiv:2605.29343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29343)).

**Transfer.** Use the drafter as its own privileged teacher. In teacher mode, it gets the true Qwen3-8B hidden states over its own drafted block as extra K/V; these are available at training time from one target forward. In student mode, it gets only verified-prefix features, or the 0.6B estimates. Train the student with reverse KL toward the teacher on student-drafted blocks (OPSD-style). Per FPD, drafts must be on-policy. Add Draft-OPD's acceptance-aware KL to the target as the distribution-matching term (the rCM lesson). SDTT-style progressive rounds can then collapse 1, 2 and 4 estimate-redraft rounds into one pass. A shortcut-style round-index embedding would let one network serve both the raw draft and the "k-round-equivalent" draft. This is the only family that aims at the 66% target-context ceiling while removing the estimator from the inference path. Its main risk is capacity: we have no evidence that 5 layers can internalize what a 0.6B pass provides. When the privileged features contain information the prefix cannot predict, the student will hedge; reverse KL is the safer choice there.

## Alignment and robust conditioning attack missing depth at training time

Representation alignment is among the largest training-efficiency levers of 2024-2026 in image diffusion. REPA regresses early DiT states onto frozen DINOv2 features and reports over 17.5x faster SiT-XL training ([arXiv:2410.06940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06940)). **REPA-E is 17x faster than REPA and 45x faster than vanilla, with ImageNet-256 FID 1.26** ([arXiv:2504.10483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10483)). REG skips the loss entirely: it appends one foundation-model token that the model denoises jointly with the latents, and **trains 63x faster than SiT-XL/2 and 23x faster than REPA at under 0.5% overhead** ([arXiv:2507.01467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01467v2)). iREPA tested 27 encoders and found that the spatial structure of the target (pairwise token similarity) matters more than its global quality ([arXiv:2512.10794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10794v1)). HASTE reports that alignment helps early in training and can hurt late, which argues for annealing it off ([arXiv:2505.16792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16792v1)). The notes found **no text or discrete-diffusion REPA paper**.

**Transfer.** Add a loss that aligns a middle drafter layer, through a small projector, to Qwen3-8B hidden states at drafted positions. That asks the drafter to predict the target computation it lacks rather than receive it, at zero inference cost. Our finding that features after a wrong token are useless means the loss should be masked after the first draft/gold divergence. iREPA suggests aligning the 16x16 token-similarity structure instead of raw per-token values, which also tolerates the estimator's noise. A REG-style variant adds one or two summary tokens to the block, with the pooled target state as their target.

Conditioning on unreliable inputs has a settled answer: corrupt the conditioning during training and tell the model how corrupted it is. Cascaded diffusion found Gaussian and blur augmentation of the low-resolution conditioning critical to sample quality ([arXiv:2106.15282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15282)). Imagen feeds the augmentation level as an input that can be selected at inference ([arXiv:2205.11487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11487)). A 2025 method learns pseudo-conditions for extremely noisy labels and reports SOTA across noise levels ([arXiv:2510.10149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10149v1)). Our result that low-bit features beat missing features is already an instance of this. **Transfer:** train on a per-position mixture of feature sources (true target, quantized target, 0.6B estimate, Gaussian-noised target, dropped) and add a per-position source or reliability embedding. Gate that reliability by the drafter's minimum confidence over earlier positions, because estimated features become worthless after the first wrong token.

Guidance offers a related, more speculative option. Autoguidance, which guides with a weaker version of the same model, beats CFG in a matched comparison: **EDM2-S ImageNet-512 FID 2.23 with CFG against 1.34 with autoguidance** ([arXiv:2510.17136](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17136v1)). Model-guidance and GFT fold guidance into training so sampling needs one pass. Model-guidance brings SiT-XL/2 to FID 1.34 without CFG ([arXiv:2502.12154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12154)). GFT matches CFG at about half the sampling cost and applies to AR and masked models ([arXiv:2501.15420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15420)). A-CFG builds the weak branch by re-masking low-confidence tokens in a masked diffusion LM and gains 3.9 points on GPQA over CFG ([arXiv:2505.20199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20199)). **Transfer:** use a GFT-style training target, logits_weak + w(logits_strong - logits_weak), where "strong" means conditioning on true target states and "weak" means conditioning on estimates or prefix only. Only the weak path runs at inference. One caution: In-situ Autoguidance, whose weak branch differs only by dropout, gave no gain (FID 2.57 against 2.56) ([arXiv:2510.17136](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17136v1)). The contrast has to be structured, and missing target depth is a structured contrast. Guidance also shifts the drafted distribution, so w must be tuned on acceptance length.

## Remasking and editing decide where to redraft, not what context to add

Discrete diffusion now has three families of self-correction. ReMDM is training-free stochastic remasking. It reaches OpenWebText MAUVE 0.656 against 0.035 for MDLM at 4096 steps, but it is slightly worse than a forward-backward corrector at 128 steps ([arXiv:2503.00307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00307v4)). That is a warning for a drafter that runs only one or two steps. Learned quality heads do better at low step counts. PRISM adds a sigmoid head trained with BCE on "was this token correct", and proves that the head converges to P(correct | context) whichever model generated the samples. It reaches **MAUVE 0.132 against ReMDM's 0.016 at 64 steps** and raises a non-fine-tuned LLaDA's HumanEval from 23.2% to 34.1% ([arXiv:2510.01384](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01384)). (This ICLR 2026 PRISM is unrelated to Ege's PRISM paper; cite with care.) RemeDi trains a policy stream on inputs corrupted with both masks and random tokens, then applies RL, lifting LLaDA-8B GSM8K from 78.3 to 89.1 and HumanEval from 45.7 to 73.2 ([arXiv:2509.23653](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23653v1)). LLaDA2.1 uses two thresholds, one to fill masks and one to edit decoded tokens. Its aggressive mode **roughly doubles tokens per forward (3.08 to 5.93 for the 100B model) at a cost of 0.09 points** ([arXiv:2602.08676](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08676)). A 300K-parameter unmasking policy trained with GRPO on confidences alone matches Fast-dLLM in semi-AR mode. Hidden-state inputs made it worse, an additive reward was hacked, and the policy transferred poorly from math to code ([arXiv:2512.09106](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09106v3)).

**Transfer.** Train a PRISM-style head on drafter states, labeled by agreement with the target token. Because acceptance is prefix-based, a hazard model that predicts the first-failure index is a natural variant. Use the head for three jobs: choosing the redraft set (the first low-quality position and everything after it), stopping the loop early, and gating the 0.6B model's corrections with a LLaDA2.1-style edit threshold. A correction that fails the gate is not injected as a token, but its hidden state can still pass as soft context. The redraft mode should be trained RemeDi-style on inputs that contain the drafter's own first-pass errors and the estimator's wrong corrections. These methods only choose positions and tokens, so on their own they live in the ~36% token-only regime. They pay off when combined with context refresh: the head decides where estimated features are still trustworthy. PRISM's guarantee assumes a clean context, and nobody has tested whether it holds when the context is a noisy estimate.

Test-time search for diffusion LMs gives moderate reasoning gains. S³ lifts LLaDA-8B MATH-500 from 25.6 to 30.2 against 28.2 for best-of-K ([arXiv:2604.06260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.06260)). Particle Gibbs dominates on classifier rewards ([arXiv:2507.08390](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08390)). Self-rewarding SMC weights particles by trajectory confidence without any external reward ([arXiv:2602.01849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01849v1)). In speculative decoding the target verifier is exact, so particle weights affect only speed. K redraft particles weighted by the quality head's predicted accepted length can be sent as a tree to one target pass. Whether that pays depends on how much tree width the target can verify per pass.

APD is the mirror image of our setup. Dream-7B drafts, and a Qwen2.5-0.5B model joins as a product of experts p_D^R * p_AR^(1-R), with shared-Gumbel coupling that accepts until the first disagreement, for **7.62 tokens per step on GSM8K** ([arXiv:2506.00413](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00413)). Our 0.6B estimator already produces next-token logits over drafted tokens. Two things follow almost for free: a product-of-experts redraft proposal, and the first drafter/0.6B disagreement index as a truncation and stopping signal.

## Caching and forecasting cut the estimator's cost, not the drafter's

Image and video caching moved from reuse to forecasting. TaylorSeer extrapolates features at skipped steps from finite differences and reaches **4.99x on FLUX with ImageReward 0.9953 against 0.9898**, and 5.00x on HunyuanVideo ([arXiv:2503.06923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06923)). SpeCa adds forecast-then-verify with a verifier that costs 1.67-3.5% of full compute and reaches **6.34x on FLUX and 7.3x on DiT** ([arXiv:2509.11628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11628v1)). dLLM caching relies on the slow drift of settled tokens: KV cosine across adjacent steps is 0.84-1.00 for the prompt in LLaDA ([arXiv:2505.22618](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22618v2)). Methods differ mainly in the refresh rule. dLLM-Cache recomputes the 25% of tokens whose Value vectors moved most, for up to 8.08x with no loss ([arXiv:2506.06295](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06295v1)). Elastic-Cache triggers on attention drift and recomputes only from the drifting layer down, since drift grows with depth, for up to 45.1x on long GSM8K ([arXiv:2510.14973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14973)). d2Cache picks a small set of tokens to recompute each step and averages 3.5x ([arXiv:2509.23094](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23094v2)). The dLLM speedups are measured against very slow vanilla dLLM decoding and do not carry over to an AR target with a normal KV cache.

**Transfer.** The 0.6B estimator is causal, so its states at positions before the first changed draft token are exactly reusable across rounds. Recompute only the suffix from that position onward, and stop iterating when that position stops moving or passes the expected acceptance length. Elastic-Cache suggests keeping the shallow layers of unchanged tokens and recomputing deep layers only. A SpeCa-style probe (a few estimator layers plus a cosine test) could decide whether a full estimate is needed at all. Forecasting the estimate across rounds (TaylorSeer on the round axis) is the weakest idea. All forecasting evidence comes from continuous denoising trajectories, while token changes make states jump discontinuously. It should be tried only for unchanged-token positions, and only after measuring cosine(h^(r), h^(r+1)).

## Ranked transfer ideas

Impact is judged against the 66% target-context ceiling and the cost of the estimator round. Novelty risk is the chance that a published or imminent paper already covers the idea.

| Rank | Idea | Sources it builds on | Expected impact | Cost | Novelty risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Privileged-teacher distillation of the estimate-redraft loop into one pass (shared-parameter drafter, teacher mode sees true target states over drafted block, on-policy reverse KL, plus Draft-OPD acceptance KL) | pi-Distill, FPD, SDTT, rCM, Draft-OPD | High: targets the 66% ceiling and removes the 0.6B pass at inference | Medium: one target forward per training block, no inference cost | Medium: Draft-OPD covers the on-policy target KL; the privileged drafter teacher and loop distillation appear unpublished |
| 2 | REPA-style alignment of drafter states at drafted positions to target states (masked after first divergence, iREPA relational variant, annealed) | REPA, REPA-E, REG, iREPA, HASTE | Medium-high: zero inference cost, direct attack on missing depth; limited by 5-layer capacity | Low | Low: no text or speculative-drafter REPA found |
| 3 | First-failure quality head that picks the redraft set, stops the loop and gates the 0.6B corrections | PRISM, RemeDi, LLaDA2.1, Learn2PD | Medium: fixes the 20% bad-correction problem and cuts wasted rounds; on its own bounded near the token-only regime | Low | Medium: quality heads are established in dLLMs, not yet in target-conditioned drafters |
| 4 | Mixed-source feature training with per-position reliability embedding, plus dual-noise (masks + own errors) redraft SFT | Cascaded diffusion, Imagen, RemeDi, Seed Diffusion | Medium: makes iteration robust and lets one drafter span oracle to none | Low | Low |
| 5 | Prefix-exact incremental estimation with depth-aware reuse and disagreement-index stopping | Fast-dLLM, dLLM-Cache, Elastic-Cache, APD | Medium on wall-clock, zero on acceptance | Low (engineering) | Low, but also low paper value alone |
| 6 | Round-index conditioning (shortcut style) so one drafter serves k = 0..K | Shortcut models, MeanFlow | Low-medium: flexibility and an anytime drafter | Low | Low |
| 7 | Particle or tree search over redraft blocks weighted by predicted accepted length | Self-rewarding SMC, S³, DFlash tree | Medium only if the target can verify wide trees cheaply | Medium-high | Medium |
| 8 | Distilled autoguidance contrast (strong vs weak context) | Autoguidance, GFT, Model-guidance, A-CFG | Uncertain; In-situ result is a warning | Low-medium | Low |
| 9 | Round-axis feature forecasting of the estimator | TaylorSeer, SpeCa | Low: smoothness untested for token streams | Low | Low |

Ideas 1 and 2 are complementary and should be built together. Alignment gives the student an internal pathway for target-like states, and privileged distillation trains it to use that pathway. Idea 3 is the cheapest experiment with an immediate payoff on the current loop, and it supplies the gating that ideas 1 and 4 need.

## Competing work the project must compare against

**Uno/UnoQwen** (2609.04010) is the main threat to the framing. It adds rank-128 gated LoRA "diffusion weights" to every layer of the frozen Qwen3-8B, so drafting shares the target's KV cache and per-layer computation, which is exactly the context our external drafter lacks. Verification is lossless. At temperature 1 it reports **acceptance 3.89 against 2.07 for DFlash and 2.08 for EAGLE-3**, per-request-optimal acceptance 5.97 against 2.74 for DFlash, 445 tok/s per request, and 0.35B extra parameters against 1.05B for DFlash ([arXiv:2609.04010](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04010)). Its DFlash numbers conflict with DFlash's own report of acceptance 5.48 at temperature 1 on Qwen3-8B ([arXiv:2602.06036](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v1)), almost certainly because of different data and thinking modes. We have to re-run both on our own setup instead of quoting either. Our argument against Uno has to be about cost and modularity (no change to target weights, a cheap external estimator), or about combining with it.

**Draft-OPD** (2605.29343) already provides on-policy, acceptance-aware training of DFlash drafters, with acceptance going from 5.35 to 5.85 and +13% speedup over DFlash ([arXiv:2605.29343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29343)). Any training-side proposal (ideas 1, 2, 4) must start from or beat a Draft-OPD-trained drafter, otherwise gains may simply be on-policy gains.

Other baselines and neighbors: **DFlash** itself ([arXiv:2602.06036](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v1)). **DiffuSpec**, a training-free DLM drafter with path search, up to 3x ([arXiv:2510.02358](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02358v1)). **S2D2**, self-speculation in block-diffusion LMs, up to 4.7x over AR on SDAR ([arXiv:2603.25702](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25702v1)), which overlaps with any pre-verifier or cascade framing. **APD**, the inverted small-model check ([arXiv:2506.00413](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00413)). **FPD**, whose draft -> partial refine -> regress loop is methodologically closest to idea 1 ([arXiv:2605.21484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21484)). DARTree (2608.13524, speculative diffusion decoding with AR draft trees) surfaced in search but was not read and needs checking before submission ([listing](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2608.13524)).

## Conclusion

Diffusion research has already solved a version of our problem several times: a fast generator with weaker information is trained to behave like a slower or better-informed process, using rollouts from the generator itself. What is new in our setting is that the missing information has a clean, cheap training-time oracle: one target forward over the drafted block. That favors training-time transfers (privileged distillation, alignment, conditioning augmentation) over test-time ones (guidance, search, forecasting), since every test-time pass competes with the latency the drafter exists to save. The estimate-redraft loop is then best seen as two things: the teacher that produces training targets, and a fallback for hard blocks. It is not the final inference path.

This also changes how the project should argue its case against Uno. If a 5-layer external drafter, distilled from a privileged teacher and aligned to target states, closes most of the 36% -> 66% gap, the result says something stronger than "iteration helps": target depth over drafted tokens can be amortized into a cheap external module without touching the target's weights. If it cannot close the gap, that negative result locates the capacity limit and makes Uno's in-target design the justified choice. Both outcomes are publishable, but only if Draft-OPD and Uno are run on the same Qwen3-8B data, thinking mode and temperature.
