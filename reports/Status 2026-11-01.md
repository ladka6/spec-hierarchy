# Status: estimating the verifier's features for in-flight tokens (1 November 2026)

Internal working document (experiment ids kept on purpose). Qwen3-8B target, DFlash z-lab/Qwen3-8B-DFlash-b16, greedy,
offline. Training data: 20k target-generated sequences (vLLM). Evaluation: 600 rounds on the 106 held-out sequences,
fixed anchors, oracle cut.

## 1. Problem (agreed)

Remove the middle verification. After the verifier gives anchor s, DFlash drafts block 1. Assume its first n tokens
are accepted, estimate the verifier's L25 / L33 features for those in-flight positions (the shallow taps can take
their mean at no loss), let the unmodified DFlash draft block 2 from them, and verify both blocks in one pass.
Estimates live only inside one round; every verification resets the context to real states. The cut n and the anchor
at the failure position (tree over DFlash's top-k) are separate, parked problems.

Training: random anchor and random span n in 1..16 over target text, teacher forcing (no cut involved).
Evaluation: n is the oracle cut (where block 1 stops matching the true text); tokens per verification = n + m2 + 1
for the chain (anchor, block 1 up to the cut, block 2). No tree in any row.

## 3. Findings

- The prize is real: real features at the in-flight positions give +62% tokens per verification.
- Drafted tokens plus a drafter trained to read them already give about half of it (lag drafter, 11.26). This is the
  bar any feature estimate has to beat; with the original DFlash, tokens give only 9.23.
- Shallow taps (L1, L9, L17) can be zero or the mean with real deep taps (8.13 / 8.04 vs 8.09 at random anchors).
  Wrong features are worse than none (all taps at the mean: 1.5 vs 3.96 removed).
- The estimator's bottleneck is the target's per-token computation, not the context (the context is in the cache).
  Random-initialised estimators plateau (exp79 variants ~5 at random anchors, exp80 ~9.65); starting from the target's
  own layers learns ~10x faster and goes further (exp81). Consistent with model-stitching results (small to large
  maps are bottlenecked by the small model) and KVShot (query estimation is the bottleneck; warm start matters).
- Untrained shortcuts fail: skipping layers without training breaks the states (exp82: only every-2nd-layer, 8.5 GB,
  stays usable at 10.76); retrieved states reach centred cosine 0.45-0.69 but help the drafter little (exp83), because
  their errors are not shaped by the drafter's needs. Cosine is a poor proxy; tau is the measure.
- The old 50M faker (cosine 0.38) was undertrained (1000 steps, loss still falling), not proof of failure.

## 4. Promising, pending budget

**exp81 continued.** Best result so far with an unmodified DFlash (10.97, bar 11.26), still rising when the learning
rate reached its floor (training zcos 0.47 / 0.43). Next run when budget allows:
- train longer (15k steps instead of 6k);
- add ground-truth cross-entropy of DFlash's block 2 (KV Prediction's base-model loss) next to the KL;
- optional: retrieved states (exp83, backoff + next) as an anchor input, KVShot-style hybrid;
- cost to address afterwards: ~2 GB per round in bf16 (5 target layers); needs ~11.5+ tokens per verification to beat
  plain DFlash in wall-clock and ~12+ to beat the lag baseline.
