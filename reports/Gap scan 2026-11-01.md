# Gap scan: block-diffusion speculative decoding (1 November 2026)

Sources: the 32 filled rows and the to-read list of the literature sheet ("Thesis research papers"), the sheet's Gaps
tab, our own measurements, and a search for the newest work on drafter training objectives. Constraints used to judge
each gap: block-diffusion drafters, efficiency, one genuinely new method (not a combination), lossless, top-venue level.

## 1. What (almost) every block-drafter paper assumes

| assumption | papers that break it | our evidence |
|---|---|---|
| drafted tokens get no target information | ReAnchor (rejected states), DLoop (drafter states), FlexDraft (target layers) | real features would give +62% tokens/verification; the information lives in the target's weights, a small estimator cannot supply it (exp79-84) |
| thinking / reasoning mode disabled, short outputs (DFlash main results, PARD-2, ACI, D-Loop, DEdit) | DFlash reports separate thinking drafters with lower tau (5.2-5.8 vs 7.9) | exp43-44: tau 3.65 in thinking mode vs 7.83 on normal answers |
| short context (training at 1-4K tokens) | LongSpark (fixed-cost drafter), DFlash long-context fine-tune | exp60-61: tau 8.8 -> 2.4 from 2K to 32K; a 1024-position window recovers 8.04 |
| one drafter per target, trained from scratch or from a checkpoint | PARD-2 target-independent mode (Qwen3 family only) | |
| fixed block size (16), one chain | DDTree (trees), D-Loop / ReAnchor (tries); adaptive length only in 2 of 12 block papers | DFlash top-1 accuracy falls from 0.93 to 0.37 along the block |
| greedy exact-match verification | DBLAST (stochastic), ReAnchor at T=1 (weaker) | |
| per-token cross-entropy training (+ position decay) | PARD-2 (target-confidence weighting), Minimizing Expected Decoding Rounds (2610.10411), Draft-OPD, Multi-Draft OPD for diffusion drafters (2610.37778, not read) | |

## 2. Candidate gaps

**G1. Block drafters on reasoning traces (thinking mode).** Every recent block-drafter paper disables thinking;
DFlash itself reports a large drop with thinking drafters, and we measured tau halving. Nobody explains why (entropy,
self-corrections, long-range dependence on earlier steps, train/test mismatch) or fixes it specifically. Highest
relevance (reasoning models are where decoding time goes). Our diagnostic tools transfer directly. Status: looks open,
needs a targeted search (SpecReason / speculative-thinking work is lossy or autoregressive).

**G2. Training objective for acceptance (idea 3).** Crowded. Variational Speculative Decoding (ICML 2026) and
Acceptance-Aware Draft Training (2609.24150) optimise expected acceptance, but for autoregressive drafters; gains are
small at greedy (+0.01-0.05 tau) and larger under sampling. For parallel / block drafters: PARD-2 (confidence-weighted
loss), 2610.10411 (minimising expected rounds), Draft-OPD and Multi-Draft OPD (on-policy distillation). A remaining
sub-gap: training a block drafter for TREE verification (DDTree notes DFlash is not trained for it; the objective would
be the accepted length of the best-first tree built from its marginals). Needs a check of 2610.10411 and 2610.37778
first.

**G3. Long-context block drafting.** Partly covered (LongSpark: fixed-cost context; DFlash: long-context fine-tune;
SpecExtend for retrieval). Our finding that the decay is attention dilution, not position extrapolation, is useful but
the method space is close to existing work.

**G4. Adaptive block length / compute per round.** Block size is fixed at 16 in every core drafter while accuracy at
positions 10-15 is low; only DiffuSpec (EOS-based) and FlexDraft (selective verification) adapt. A drafter that decides
how many positions to draft (or where to stop) per round. Medium novelty, modest ceiling.

**G5. Verification rule for block drafters.** Almost all use greedy exact match. Lossless sequence-level verification
(block verification, hierarchical verification) has not been studied with block drafters, whose per-position marginals
are independent. Ceiling probably small at T=0, larger at T>0.

**G6. Target information for drafted tokens (our line so far).** Addressed by ReAnchor, DLoop, FlexDraft; our results
show the remaining gain needs target computation. Best result: exp81 (5 target layers, ~2 GB/round, 10.97 vs bar 11.26),
parked as promising.

## 3. Assessment

| gap | open? | relevance | ceiling | fits "one new method" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 reasoning traces | likely | very high | large (tau halves today) | yes, once the cause is known |
| G2 acceptance objective | crowded; tree-objective sub-gap maybe open | medium | small at T=0 | yes |
| G3 long context | partly | high | large at long context | moderate |
| G4 adaptive block length | partly | medium | modest | yes |
| G5 verification rule | open for block drafters | medium | small at T=0 | yes |
| G6 target info for drafted tokens | partly; hard | high | 1.24x at 8B (k=5 ceiling) | exp81 |

Recommendation: G1 first (diagnose where block drafters lose acceptance on reasoning traces, then design the fix), with
the tree-aware training objective (G2 sub-gap) as the candidate if a check of 2610.10411 / 2610.37778 shows it is open.
