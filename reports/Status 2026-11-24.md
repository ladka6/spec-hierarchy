# Status: what the drafter reads, the band pass, and the token at the cut (24 November 2026)

Internal working document (experiment ids kept on purpose). Everything below was measured on 23 and 24 November 2026.
Unless stated otherwise: Qwen3-8B target, z-lab/Qwen3-8B-DFlash-b16 (block 16, taps = target layers 1 / 9 / 17 / 25 /
33), greedy, offline, sequences from `hspec_lagft/data`, held-out anchors of exp112 (600, or 2000 for exp117).
tau = accepted drafted tokens + 1. "Accepted drafted tokens" excludes the bonus token.
On this data one DFlash block gives tau 5.27 (4.26 drafted tokens); the 106 held-out sequences of exp81 give 8.50, so
absolute numbers must not be compared across the two data sets.

Scripts: `scripts/exp111_tap_importance.py`, `exp113_shallow_verify.py`, `exp114_draftable_target.py`,
`exp115_tap_content.py`, `exp116_band_bridge.py`, `exp117_cut_token.py`, data for the cross-family runs from
`scripts/gen_pairdata.py`. Jobs in `snellius/` (`job_taps`, `job_content`, `job_exp113` .. `job_exp117`).

## 1. Where the line stood

A chain of blocks before one full verify gives about L (1 - f^B) / (1 - f) drafted tokens, with L the drafted tokens
of one block and f the probability that the chain survives a cut (the cut is found AND the token there is right).
f decides how deep a chain pays. Two separate needs follow: features for the in-flight tokens, and the token at the
cut (in plain DFlash that token is the bonus of the verify pass).

## 2. The decision at the cut from cheap target computation does not work

### exp113: shape of a shallow verify at a fixed budget

Sub-stack of frozen target layers over the in-flight tokens, own real prefix cache, ending in the target's final norm
and lm_head. Bridges over removed spans (closed-form affine + trained MLP). f = first disagreement with the draft
exactly at the drafter's first error and the token right; "cand" = choice among the drafter's top 16.

| first | 8 | 0-7 | 0.547 | 0.168 | 0.038 | 0.157 | 0.347 |
| sandwich | 4 | 0,1,34,35 | 0.599 | 0.184 | 0.054 | 0.187 | 0.313 |
| sandwich | 8 | 0-3,32-35 | 0.740 | 0.268 | 0.101 | 0.209 | 0.277 |
| skip | 4 | 0,12,23,35 | 0.655 | 0.225 | 0.090 | 0.186 | 0.323 |
| skip | 8 (cost 9.1) | 0,5,..,35 | 0.776 | 0.285 | 0.126 | 0.213 | 0.267 |
| first + LoRA r64 | 4 / 8 | | 0.563 / 0.613 | 0.184 / 0.211 | | 0.155 / 0.162 | |
| sandwich + LoRA r64 | 4 / 8 | | 0.659 / 0.784 | 0.202 / 0.286 | | 0.180 / 0.220 | |

Reference on the same anchors: the drafter's own second choice is right at 0.389 of the first errors (location given),
the target's token is among its top 16 at 0.892. Best two-block chain over all margins: 5.08 accepted drafted tokens

The attention-only shape was still running when this was written.

### exp114: can the backbone be made draftable by a short training?

LoRA r32 on all backbone layers, exit loss at 4 and 8 layers (KL to the model's own final output through a reader),
drift term lam * KL(original || new). M' = M + LoRA; truth for the evaluation is M' itself.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| frozen (readers only) | 4000 | 1.000 | 0.424 / 0.466 | 0.166 | 0.182 | 5.27 / 5.26 |
| LoRA, lam 10 | 4000 | 0.965 | 0.466 / 0.506 | 0.180 | 0.210 | 5.27 / 5.28 |
| LoRA, lam 1 | 4000 | 0.946 | 0.475 / 0.518 | 0.185 | 0.208 | 5.27 / 5.28 |
| LoRA, lam 0.1 | 4000 | 0.937 | 0.477 / 0.522 | 0.190 | 0.213 | 5.27 / 5.20 |
| LoRA, lam 1 | 12000 | 0.948 | 0.508 / 0.558 | 0.211 | 0.217 | 5.27 / 5.20 |

Readings: 3.5 to 6% of the greedy tokens change for +0.02 to +0.03 in f; loosening the drift constraint 100 times buys
nothing; three times the training buys +0.01 to +0.03. Side result: a KL-constrained backbone LoRA of this size does
not hurt the original drafter. Only one recipe was tried (self-distillation exit loss, 2M to 6M tokens, no layer
dropout); drift was measured as KL and top-1 agreement only.

about the past. The reason is open. Gemma has a zone (layers 24 to 29) where both readouts drop; its tap in that zone
(L27) slightly hurts the drafter when present.

## 4. The band pass (exp116)

Hypothesis from section 3: for the in-flight tokens only the band L/2 .. 3L/4 has to run; what comes before it is
bridged; the band layers read the real cache of the verified prefix at their own depth; the output is the drafter's
3/4 tap. Evaluation as exp111: the last m positions get only that tap (from the sub-stack), the other taps their mean.
"of 1 tap" = (tau - no features) / (real tap - no features). References: all taps real 5.27, no features for the last
8 positions 1.78, only the real L25 tap 4.01.

Bridge kind, band shape (layers 0, 1, bridge, 18-25; 10 layers):

| bridge | cosine to the real tap | tau m8 | of 1 tap m4 / m8 / m16 |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.09 | 1.35 | below no features |
| affine, closed form | 0.68 | 3.23 | 0.78 / 0.65 / 0.61 |
| + MLP | 0.86 | 3.73 | 0.95 / 0.87 / 0.87 |
| + self-attention over the in-flight tokens | 0.86 | 3.78 | 0.98 / 0.90 / 0.90 |
| + attention to the real prefix states at the output depth | 0.86 | 3.77 | 0.96 / 0.89 / 0.86 |

Slice at the same budget (MLP bridges):

| slice | layers | of 1 tap m8 (10 layers) | of 1 tap m8 (6 layers) |
|---|---|---|---|
| band, 2 shallow layers | 0,1,18-25 / 0,1,22-25 | 0.87 | 0.66 |
| band, 4 shallow layers | 0-3,20-25 | 0.83 | |
| band, no shallow layer | 16-25 | 0.78 | |
| plateau | 0,1,10-17 | 0.65 | |
| evenly spread | 0,3,..,25 / 0,5,..,25 | 0.49 | 0.62 |
| first layers | 0-9 / 0-5 | 0.47 | 0.33 |

Block similarity (cosine between the states before and after a block of 8 layers): 0.16 for layers 0-7, 0.64 to 0.77
for every block in between, 0.35 for layers 28-35. The plateau is not flatter than the band by this criterion.

Readings: a bridge is needed and a simple one is enough (affine + MLP). At equal budget the band gives almost twice
what the first layers or an even spread give. The advantage shrinks at 6 layers. Not to be compared with exp112
(share 0.67 to 0.73 at 4 layers): there a reader predicted all taps and was trained through the drafter; here one
tap is supplied and the bridge is trained on the state. Against all taps real, the band's tau 3.78 is 0.57 of the way
from no features (the real tap alone is 0.64). Qwen only so far. The justification by the usual similarity criterion
does not hold; the equal-budget comparison does.

| target | share | fix | masked | among first 3 (masked) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| | real features | no features |

even with the real state. All of this assumes the exact error position; with a real cut rule a correct token may be
excluded. Not yet measured with band features.

## 6. Cost (not measured, model only)

With the timings of `docs/method_partial_pass.md` (drafter 3.3 ms, verify 19 ms) a chain stage of the band (28% of a
pass, 5.3 ms) plus a second drafter call is 8.6 ms, 39% of a normal round. With f = 0.54 a second block returns about
2.3 drafted tokens (3.7 ms per token against 4.2 ms per token for a normal round); with shallow-feature fix rates it
is slower than a normal round. The levers are a cheaper stage, a higher f (section 5), or a larger target. The Qwen
timings of the chain were never measured end to end.

### Where this leaves step 2

- Training the drafter for the in-flight pattern is the largest single lever measured so far.
- Two things are needed from the target side: the place of the first error (starts the chain) and features for the
  kept tokens (decide how deep it goes). The drafter's own confidence gives neither depth nor a gain.
- Budget: about 2 ms per stage (three to four layers) keeps 1.4x of the 1.65x; ten layers do not pay.
- Not measured: features of intermediate quality inside the chain, a learned cut detector, other families, the
  drafter trained together with a refitted single-layer interface.

## 9. Speculative features: how much must the in-flight feature carry once the drafter is trained to read it (exp125)

Idea under test: features for the in-flight tokens need not be the target's real states and need not be good for
language modelling; a model may produce speculative features, the drafter is trained to work with them (dual mode:
the in-flight tokens stay as inputs), the chain runs on them and one lossless verify ends the round. First question:
how much information the drafter needs at those positions when everything is trained for the drafter's own loss.

Setup: start = lag drafter; in-flight positions read a learned r-dimensional code of their real fused feature
(starts as the rank-r PCA projection, plus zero-initialised nonlinear parts), encoder, decoder and drafter trained
on the block loss only, 1 epoch (5610 steps). Held-out sequences, tau = accepted drafted tokens + 1, one block.

| in-flight positions read | lag 4 | lag 8 | lag 16 | share of the gap (4 / 8 / 16) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tokens only (same extra training) | 4.89 | 4.38 | 3.71 | 0 |
| code, r = 16 | 5.01 | 4.70 | 4.03 | 0.18 / 0.23 / 0.18 |
| code, r = 64 | 5.12 | 5.05 | 4.45 | 0.35 / 0.48 / 0.41 |
| code, r = 256 | 5.38 | 5.44 | 4.89 | 0.74 / 0.75 / 0.66 |
| real features | 5.55 | 5.78 | 5.51 | 1 |

Standard blocks (lag 0) stay at 5.66 to 5.71 in every arm.

Before training (the PCA projection on the lag drafter, lag 8): r = 16: 2.76, r = 64: 3.86, r = 256: 4.95. The
first two are below tokens only (4.26): a partial feature the drafter was not trained for is worse than none.

- The lag drafter is not undertrained: one more epoch moves tokens-only from 4.26 to 4.38 at lag 8.
- Real features on top of the tokens remove the lag almost entirely (5.51 at lag 16 against 5.66 without lag); the
  value of in-flight features grows with the lag (0.66, 1.40, 1.80 tokens at lag 4, 8, 16).
- Trained for the drafter's loss, 64 numbers per in-flight token close about half of the gap and 256 three
  quarters; the curve is smooth in r, no knee.
- A first version with a zero-initialised code from the raw taps stayed at the baseline in all arms: the drafter
  learned to ignore the rows (optimisation failure, not a result).

Related earlier results on the same question: exp84 (frozen drafter, rank-r linear subspace of the real deep
features: 256 dimensions 0.68 of the ceiling, 1024 0.92, drafter-Fisher subspace no better than PCA), exp80 (separate
estimator, frozen drafter, imitation loss: plateau below the lag drafter), exp81 (target tap layers + LoRA +
bridges, frozen drafter: 10.97 tokens per verification against 11.26 for the lag drafter), exp85 (first 10 real
layers, drafter trained for them: 11.56).

Open: the code is computed from the real feature (upper bound); next is a cheap producer of it (a sparse path
through the backbone, about 2 ms per stage), trained in the same single training; a one-stage run from the original
DFlash drafter to check that the lag pre-training is not needed as a separate step; the chain (exp124) on these
checkpoints (`/scratch-shared/$USER/hspec_code/`).
