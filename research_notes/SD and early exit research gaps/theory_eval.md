## Q1. Which theoretical questions are open (predicting acceptance / optimal tree size from drafter-target statistics without decoding; limits of parallel vs AR drafters)?

### Takeaway
The theory is mature for single-draft and multi-draft *distribution-preserving* verification (Markov-chain view, optimal-transport multi-draft optimum, block verification, polybasic chains), and a 2026 paper started certifying *deterministic/greedy* acceptance via KL thresholds. What is still missing is a predictive theory that maps cheap, offline drafter/target statistics (divergence profile, hidden-state similarity, entropy distribution, layer redundancy) to acceptance length, optimal tree budget and end-to-end speedup, and any formal treatment of parallel (block-diffusion) drafters vs AR drafters.

### Cited Findings
Verification / acceptance theory
- Yin, Chen, Huang, Wang (NeurIPS 2024), "A Theoretical Perspective for Speculative Decoding Algorithm", arXiv 2411.00841: models SD as a Markov chain, analyses theoretical limits of acceleration, batch SD, and the quality/speed trade-off; efficiency is governed by total variation distances between the component models. — [arXiv 2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- Polybasic SD (ICML 2025), arXiv 2510.26527: proves a "fundamental theorem" for the optimal inference time of multi-model (more than two models) speculative chains, jointly in model capability, acceptance rate and cost; reports 3.31-4.01x on LLaMA2-Chat 7B, up to 3.87x LLaMA3-8B, 4.43x Vicuna-7B, 3.85x Qwen2-7B, distribution-preserving. — [arXiv 2510.26527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26527); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=n7iwmPacDt)
- "Towards Optimal Multi-Draft Speculative Decoding" (ICLR 2025), arXiv 2502.18779: recasts the optimal multi-draft acceptance rate (previously exponential) as subset selection using optimal transport and total unimodularity, computable in O(|V| log |V|); first measurement of the optimum at real vocab sizes; existing verifiers (RRS, K-SEQ) sit 1-3% below the optimum; sampling without replacement beats with replacement; a greedy draft construction (top n-1 + one sampled) is optimal in some regimes. — [arXiv 2502.18779](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18779)
- Block verification, Sun et al., arXiv 2403.10444 (ICML 2024 workshop / later venue): verifies the draft block jointly rather than token by token, provably optimal among valid verification schemes for a single draft. [Details not fetched; title/venue from search results only] — [arXiv 2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=frsg32u0rO)
- SpecTr (optimal-transport multi-draft, Sun et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2310.15141) and Leviathan et al. 2023 (arXiv 2211.17192, expected accepted tokens (1-alpha^(k+1))/(1-alpha) under i.i.d. acceptance) are the pre-2024 foundations. [UNVERIFIED in this session, from background knowledge]
- "When Is a Draft Accepted? A Theory of Acceptance in Speculative Decoding", arXiv 2606.30265 (June 2026): shows greedy, additive/multiplicative-margin relaxed, top-m and entropy-threshold acceptance rules all have rejection regions that are lower level sets of the target distribution; derives exact KL-divergence thresholds (tight lower/upper bounds) that certify single-token acceptance, and extends to tree verification with branching factor m (log(m+1) upper bounds); validated on Qwen3; focuses on "deterministic local acceptance events common in practical inference systems". — [arXiv HTML 2606.30265v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30265v1)
- "Scaling Laws for Speculative Decoding" (Scylla), arXiv 2505.07858: log-linear laws for acceptance vs drafter pretraining tokens, drafter capacity and batch size; claims 1.5-2.2 higher acceptance length than EAGLE-2 and +0.3 over EAGLE-3 at T=0, 2x throughput over EAGLE-2 in production. — [arXiv 2505.07858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07858)
- Variational Speculative Decoding (arXiv 2602.05774) argues drafter training should target sequence acceptance rather than token likelihood [title/snippet only, not fetched]. — [arXiv 2602.05774](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05774v1)
- "Speculative Speculative Decoding" (Kumar, Dao, May), arXiv 2603.03251, speculates on verification outcomes to parallelise drafting and verification [snippet only]. — [arXiv 2603.03251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03251)
- A search for "How Speculative Can Speculative Decoding Be" returned no paper with that exact title (closest hits were 2603.03251 and "Decoding Speculative Decoding" 2402.01528). [Title could not be verified; possibly misremembered] — [search results incl. arXiv 2402.01528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01528)

Representation-based acceptance
- "Make Every Draft Count: Hidden State based Speculative Decoding", arXiv 2602.21224 [title from search; content not fetched]. — [arXiv 2602.21224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21224)
- "Attention Drift: What Autoregressive Speculative Decoding Models Learn", arXiv 2605.09992: pre-norm EAGLE-3 drafters behave like extra transformer layers stacked on the target, not independent AR models; hidden-state norm grows monotonically with speculation depth and attention migrates from the sink to recent drafted tokens (also in MTP heads); pre-norm drafters lose up to 52% acceptance accuracy under chat-template perturbation vs 5% for post-norm; post-norm + RMSNorm before fusion gives up to 2x under template perturbation and 1.18x on long context. — [arXiv 2605.09992](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09992v1)
- "When Hidden States Drift: Can KV Caches Rescue Long-Range Speculative Decoding?", arXiv 2604.26412 [title only]. — [Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2604.26412)

Parallel vs AR drafters
- DFlash (arXiv 2602.06036) block-diffusion drafter; xPress (2608.02438) parallel refinement for diffusion drafters; Domino (2605.29707) decoupling causal modeling from AR drafting [titles from search, not fetched]. — [DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v2); [xPress](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02438); [Domino](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29707v1)
- DPara, arXiv 2609.27396: argues parallel logit prediction alone gives incoherent sequences, so DSpark-style methods add a light AR head; DPara precomputes draft representations for every possible acceptance boundary to remove fallback; 3.21x/3.52x on Qwen3-8B/14B; no formal theory of parallel vs AR drafter limits. — [arXiv 2609.27396](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27396v1)
- Negative result, arXiv 2607.12422 (Javat, Kazakov): LoRA block-diffusion drafter on shared Qwen3-0.6B backbone gets longer accepted prefixes (2.881/round) but 34 tok/s vs FastMTP 188 tok/s because each round costs two full-backbone passes: "accepted tokens are not free". — [arXiv 2607.12422](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12422v1)
- JetSpec (arXiv 2606.18394) claims to "break the scaling ceiling" of SD with parallel tree drafting [title only]. — [arXiv 2606.18394](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18394)

Early exit (analysis)
- "The Diminishing Returns of Early-Exit Decoding in Modern LLMs", arXiv 2603.23701 (Wei, Du, Yu, Tiwari, Li, Xu, Wang): defines the Early-Exit Adaptability Score (EAS), a weighted geometric mean of skip ratio (L-l)/L and layer-to-final similarity (hidden-state cosine, logits, or top-K), averaged over exit layers; EAS approx 0.52 Llama2-7B, 0.51 Qwen3-8B, 0.46 Llama3-8B; with *oracle* exit selection and a 5% accuracy tolerance modern base models only skip 0.04-7.26% of layers; dense > MoE and SSM; >20B models more suitable; base > instruct; Pythia checkpoints show more pretraining reduces suitability. Only oracle analysis, not trained early-exit models; links to SD (SpecEE) only in passing. — [arXiv 2603.23701](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23701v1)
- Self-speculative layer-skip work continues regardless (ConfLayers 2604.14612, KnapSpec 2602.20217, CLaSp ACL 2025) [titles from search, not fetched]. — [ConfLayers](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14612v1); [KnapSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20217v1); [CLaSp](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1525.pdf)

### Inferences
- Distribution-preserving verification is close to solved: the multi-draft optimum is only 1-3% above current verifiers (2502.18779), so incremental verification-rule papers have little headroom. The open theory is on the *drafter side* and on *prediction*: nobody (found) gives a closed-form or cheaply estimable predictor of expected accepted length / optimal tree budget from offline statistics of (drafter, target, data) without running speculative decoding.
- 2606.30265 gives per-token KL certificates but not an aggregated predictor of acceptance length over a trajectory; turning its certificates plus the empirical distribution of target margins/entropies into a predicted tau and optimal tree size is a natural, small-compute follow-up.
- Attention Drift (2605.09992) implies EAGLE-style drafters are "extra target layers", which links SD acceptance to layer redundancy, the very quantity 2603.23701 measures with EAS. No paper found connects EAS-type layer-redundancy measures to self-speculative or EAGLE acceptance across model generations.
- For parallel drafters, the empirical story (need an AR correction head, DPara; compute asymmetry matters, 2607.12422) has no formal counterpart. A theory of "joint vs product-of-marginals" acceptance (block drafters predict per-position marginals; acceptance depends on the target's sequence-level dependence, e.g. conditional mutual information between adjacent tokens) looks open.

Candidate gaps (theory) with novelty check
1. "Predicting speculative acceptance length and optimal tree budget from offline drafter/target statistics" (TV/KL profile, target margin distribution, CKA/cosine of hidden states). Closest: 2606.30265 (per-token KL certificates, no trajectory-level predictor), 2505.07858 (scaling laws in drafter size/data, not per-pair prediction), 2502.18779 (computes optimum given distributions, requires running both models). Direct searches for "predict acceptance rate without decoding" and "hidden state similarity predicts acceptance" returned only blogs and 2602.21224/2606.30265. Status: appears open. Feasible on 1-4 A100 with 8B targets.
2. "Layer redundancy (EAS) as a predictor of self-speculative / EAGLE acceptance across model generations." Closest: 2603.23701 (EAS, no SD acceptance), 2605.09992 (drafters as extra layers). No direct hit found. Status: appears open.
3. "Acceptance limits of parallel (block-diffusion) drafters vs AR drafters as a function of target token dependence." Closest: DPara 2609.27396, 2607.12422, DFlash 2602.06036; none theoretical. Status: appears open but needs careful literature check of DFlash/xPress appendices (not fetched).

## Q2. Which evaluation practices are known to be misleading, and is there a gap for a careful measurement study (including numerical losslessness in bf16)?

### Takeaway
Three evaluation problems are now documented: (a) research-prototype, batch-1 HF speedups do not transfer to production engines at realistic batch sizes; (b) low-diversity or synthetic benchmark inputs overestimate speedups; (c) "lossless" is only true in exact arithmetic, since bf16 runs diverge from plain greedy on roughly half of prompts for one method (Orthrus) and batch SD implementations have broken equivalence outright. No study yet measures bf16 trajectory divergence across the mainstream SD families (EAGLE-3, Medusa/MTP, draft-model, DFlash/DDTree trees) with batch-invariant kernels as a control, which matches the user's own observation (15-19/40 divergent outputs for every method).

### Cited Findings
Benchmarks and speedup reporting
- Spec-Bench (Xia et al., ACL Findings 2024, arXiv 2401.07851): unified batch-1, HF-style benchmark with six subtasks. [UNVERIFIED, background knowledge; not fetched]
- "Speculative Decoding: Performance or Illusion?" (Liu, Yu, Park, Stoica, Cheung), arXiv 2601.11580: first systematic SD study in vLLM; 5 methods (n-gram, EAGLE, EAGLE-3, draft model, MTP), 4 targets (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3-70B, Qwen3-8B, GLM-4.5-Air), 6 workloads, batch 1-128; EAGLE on Llama-3-70B drops from 1.96x at batch 1 to 1.21x at batch 128; verification is 42-95% of execution time; EAGLE-3 best overall, n-gram only wins for code editing; large gap to an oracle adaptive policy. — [SpecDecode-Bench site](https://specdecode-bench.github.io/); [arXiv 2601.11580](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.11580)
- SPEED-Bench (NVIDIA), arXiv 2604.09557: critiques prior benchmarks for limited task diversity, no throughput-regime evaluation, and non-production implementations; finds batch-size-dependent optimal draft length, "synthetic inputs overestimate real-world throughput", biases from low-diversity data, and caveats of vocabulary pruning in SOTA drafters; integrates vLLM and TensorRT-LLM. — [arXiv 2604.09557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09557); [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/speed-bench)
- A benchmark of SD for test-time scaling exists on OpenReview ("Scaling Up, Speeding Up") [title only]. — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=DjOmnwX4wJ)

Correctness / losslessness
- "Batch Speculative Decoding Done Right", arXiv 2510.22876: prior batch SD systems (BSP, DSD) violate output equivalence because of ragged tensors (position IDs, masks, KV cache desync), with near-zero exact match vs reference at batch >1 despite good throughput numbers; EqSpec/EXSpec restore correctness, with alignment overhead up to 40%; they reach about 95% decoding equivalence on SpecBench, attributing the residual 5% to floating-point nondeterminism. — [arXiv 2510.22876](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22876v3)
- "How Lossless Is Lossless Speculative Decoding? The Role of Numerical Precision in Orthrus" (Koziev, Sinev, Oseledets), arXiv 2609.15504: reproduces Orthrus (AR backbone + diffusion head, 1.7B); under BF16 exact trajectory match with greedy AR in only 45% (original checkpoint) / 43% (retrained) of 1,190 prompts; FP32 gives 100% match; divergence correlates strongly with response-conditional perplexity (beta1 -8.10 to -10.92, p<0.001); no systematic downstream degradation on GSM8K/HumanEval/IFEval. Only Orthrus at 1.7B; no EAGLE/Medusa; no batch-invariant kernels; the specific operations causing divergence left as future work. — [arXiv 2609.15504](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15504v1)
- "Greedy Decoding Is Not Precision-Invariant", arXiv 2609.26621: same model/prompt/greedy gives different outputs in BF16 vs FP16 on identical hardware for 49-100% of prompts, six models 1.1B-7B; selective FP32 LM-head recomputation when the top-1/top-2 margin is below a threshold adds +22-36 points exact agreement at under 4% latency (low batch). Does not address SD. — [awesomepapers entry for 2609.26621](https://awesomepapers.io/llm-papers/papers/2609.26621)
- Thinking Machines, "Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference" (Sep 2025): root cause is lack of batch invariance in kernels, not concurrency per se; Qwen3-235B at T=0, 1,000 runs gave 80 unique completions (identical for first 102 tokens); batch-invariant kernels gave 1,000 identical outputs; Qwen3-8B 1,000 sequences: 26 s default vLLM, 55 s naive deterministic, 42 s with improved attention kernel. No mention of SD. — [Thinking Machines blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/)
- vLLM batch-invariance mode: beta, NVIDIA CC>=8.0 (so A100 works) and Intel XPU, disables custom all-reduce etc.; docs do not mention SD compatibility. — [vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/batch_invariance/)
- SGLang deterministic inference blog and batch-invariant ops issue. [Not fetched] — [LMSYS blog](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-09-22-sglang-deterministic/); [SGLang issue 10278](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/10278)
- LLM-42 (Microsoft Research et al.), arXiv 2601.17768: gets determinism by *reusing* SD's decode-verify-rollback: fast non-invariant decode, replay under fixed shape, rollback mismatches; reports batch-invariant GEMM 53% slower and RMSNorm up to 50% slower than optimized kernels; within 3% of peak at 2% deterministic traffic, under 11% recompute at 100%. — [arXiv 2601.17768](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17768v1)

### Inferences
- The user's result (every method, including DDTree, diverges from greedy in 15-19 of 40 outputs, about 38-48%) is in the same range as the Orthrus BF16 number (45-55% divergence) and the BF16 vs FP16 study (49-100%), which suggests the divergence is a property of bf16 greedy decoding near ties, not of any one SD method. This is a publishable framing: "lossless" SD claims are underspecified, and SD-vs-AR divergence should be compared to the AR-vs-AR noise floor (AR at different batch sizes / kernels), which no SD paper found does.
- There is a clean experimental design available on A100 (vLLM batch-invariant mode supports CC 8.0): measure divergence (a) AR vs AR across batch sizes, (b) SD vs AR default kernels, (c) SD vs AR with batch-invariant kernels, (d) FP32 LM head or FP32 overall; attribute divergence to verification-shape changes (verifying k tokens changes GEMM shapes, same mechanism as batch variance); relate divergence to target margin distribution (links to 2606.30265 and 2609.26621's margin trigger); and test whether divergence changes accuracy (Orthrus found none).
- Reporting practices known to be misleading, with sources: batch-1 only (2601.11580), HF prototypes vs production engines (2601.11580, 2604.09557), synthetic / low-diversity prompts (2604.09557), throughput numbers from implementations that are not actually equivalent (2510.22876), and "lossless" without stating precision (2609.15504).

## Q3. Is there a gap around fairness/robustness of SD across languages, domains, or user groups (disparate impact), privacy, or robustness under distribution shift and target updates?

### Takeaway
Disparate impact across tasks/languages is established (2510.02128; 2605.30580) and privacy leakage via speculation-dependent packet patterns is established (2411.01076). Drafter staleness under target change is documented for RL (ReSpec 2510.26475) and deployment perturbations (Attention Drift 2605.09992), but no paper found gives a systematic, quantitative study of how much acceptance is lost when the target is fine-tuned (SFT/LoRA/domain adaptation), how it depends on the size of the weight or distribution shift, and how cheaply it can be recovered. The user's own finding (10-35% acceptance loss, cheaply recoverable) fits this gap directly.

### Cited Findings
Disparate impact / languages
- "The Disparate Impacts of Speculative Decoding" (Sandler et al.), arXiv 2510.02128: speedups are not uniform; under-fit and underrepresented tasks get less acceleration; Qwen2.5 0.5B drafter / 3B verifier on MGSM shows a 13% gap in mean acceptance between fastest and slowest languages (Japanese slowest and least accurate); fairness metric = dispersion of drafter-target cross-entropy divergence across tasks; monotone chain divergence -> acceptance -> speedup; mitigation s-CDF (stochastic corrective drafter finetuning) gives about 12% average improvement in the fairness metric. Small models only (0.5B/3B). — [arXiv 2510.02128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02128); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02128v1); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=2hOQcn5QUA)
- "Speculative Decoding Across Languages" (Paudel, Ginn, De Nardi, Palmer), arXiv 2605.30580: 11 low-resource languages; translation acceptance 0.40 baseline (0.30-0.54), 0.60 with task-specific distillation, 0.39 general distillation, 0.24 n-gram; distillation gives 1.28x in-domain but only 1.03x on held-out story generation; n-gram drafters 1.30-1.39x because of near-zero cost. Uses small draft/verifier models, not EAGLE-style. — [arXiv HTML 2605.30580v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30580v1)

Privacy
- "When Speculation Spills Secrets: Side Channels via Speculative Decoding in LLMs" (Univ. of Toronto), arXiv 2411.01076: packet-size patterns reveal per-iteration accepted counts; query fingerprinting on a 50-query set: REST 100% (T=0.3), BiLD 95.2%, LADE 91.6%, EAGLE 77.6% on remote vLLM; still well above 2% chance at T=1.0; also leaks REST datastore at >25 tokens/s; mitigations: random padding (-70% accuracy, 8.7x payload), token aggregation (-50%), fixed-size padding (-98%, 230x payload). — [arXiv 2411.01076](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.01076)
- Related non-SD timing channels: "Time Will Tell" (arXiv 2412.15431), Whisper Leak (Microsoft, arXiv 2511.03675), "Leaky Language Models: Stealing Architecture and Inference Optimizations via Per-Token Timing" (2607.20723) [titles only]. — [2412.15431](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15431v1); [Microsoft blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2025/11/07/whisper-leak-a-novel-side-channel-cyberattack-on-remote-language-models/); [Pith 2607.20723](https://pith.science/paper/2607.20723)

Robustness to target updates / distribution shift
- ReSpec, arXiv 2510.26475: in RL training, EAGLE-3 acceptance length declines as the policy updates, "significantly within just 100 training steps"; fix is online on-policy distillation of the drafter from the actor. — [arXiv 2510.26475](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26475)
- Attention Drift, arXiv 2605.09992: large acceptance losses under chat-template changes (up to 52% for pre-norm drafters), long contexts beyond training length, and system-prompt length shifts. — [arXiv 2605.09992](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09992v1)
- Domain drafter training best practices, arXiv 2503.07807; OmniDraft online adaptive cross-vocabulary drafter, arXiv 2507.02659; Draft-OPD on-policy distillation for drafters, arXiv 2605.29343; vLLM RFC for LoRA adapters on DFlash drafters (issue 52038) [titles/snippets only]. — [2503.07807](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07807v1); [2507.02659](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02659v1); [2605.29343](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29343v1); [vLLM issue 52038](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/52038)

### Inferences
- Disparate impact has so far been shown with small classic draft models (0.5B/3B Qwen2.5; small models on low-resource languages). Whether the dominant 2025-2026 drafters (EAGLE-3, MTP heads, DFlash/DDTree trees) at 8B show the same or larger disparity, and whether tree drafting narrows it, is unmeasured in what was found. The "drafter = extra target layers" result suggests feature-level drafters might inherit target competence and show *smaller* disparity; that is testable.
- Target-update robustness: evidence is either RL-specific (ReSpec) or about input-format shift (Attention Drift). A controlled "drafter transfer" study (same drafter, target fine-tuned by SFT, LoRA, DPO, domain continued pretraining at varying strengths; measure acceptance loss vs KL(target_new || target_old) and vs weight distance; measure how many retraining tokens/steps recover it) would tie into Q1's predictor and the user's 10-35% observation.
- Privacy: the side channel is proportional to the variance of accepted length, which is exactly what disparate impact and tree drafters change. No paper found evaluates leakage for tree/parallel drafters or for per-language disparities (e.g., can the attacker infer the language/domain of a query from acceptance patterns). This is analysis-driven and cheap.

8. "Disparate impact of feature-level and tree/parallel drafters (EAGLE-3, DFlash, DDTree) across languages and domains at 8B." Closest: 2510.02128 (Qwen2.5 0.5B/3B draft-model SD), 2605.30580 (low-resource translation, small models). Status: open for modern drafters.
