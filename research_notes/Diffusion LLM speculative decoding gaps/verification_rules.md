# Verification rules, lossless sampling, and tree construction for parallel / block / diffusion drafts

Scope: 2024-2026, emphasis on 2026. Research done 2026-11-02. Numbers are copied as the papers state them. Where a detail came only from an abstract or a partial page, it is marked [abstract only] or [partial text]. Limitations are only what each paper reports.

## Q1. Which lossless sampling rules exist for multi-draft and tree drafts, and do any target conditionally independent block/diffusion draft positions? Has anyone derived the optimal acceptance for that case?

### Takeaway
There is a well-developed line of lossless rules for (a) single-step multi-draft (OT-optimal acceptance, canonical decomposition, Global Resolution), (b) single-chain multi-step (Block Verification, HSD), and (c) trees (Traversal Verification, UniVer, Layer Verification). All of them assume drafts sampled from an autoregressive draft model, either i.i.d. or with a specific top-(m-1)+1 mixed sampling. I found no paper that derives an optimal lossless acceptance rule for draft trees built from conditionally independent per-position marginals (DFlash/DDTree-style). The block-diffusion tree papers (DDTree, BASTION, CaDDTree, PRESTO, DARTree) all build deterministic top-B trees and reuse an inherited "walk the tree with the target's decoding rule" verifier, and none analyses losslessness or optimality at T>0 separately.

### Cited Findings

Single-step multi-draft (one position, n candidate tokens):
- SpecTr / OT framing is the base. "Towards Optimal Multi-Draft Speculative Decoding" (Hu et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv:2502.18779, Feb 2025) writes optimal acceptance as an OT problem, takes the LP dual, and gets α* = 1 + min_H [P(H) − Q(H)]. For with- and without-replacement sampling the set function is "q-convex", so α* is computable by sort plus linear scan (O(|Σ| log|Σ|), plus O(n|Σ|) DP without replacement). Assumes single-step only (multi-step tree decoding left to future work) and p(x) > 0 for all tokens — [Hu et al. 2025](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18779)
- Same paper: existing verifiers fall short of the bound. OPT-6.7B/125M on Alpaca: RRS with replacement 1.5 points below optimum, K-SEQ with replacement 1.1 points below, RRS without replacement 0.9 points below. The gap grows with temperature and number of drafts. Sampling without replacement gives higher acceptance than with replacement. Their greedy draft construction (top n−1 deterministic, last one sampled) nearly matches the bound (e.g. 84.2 vs 84.3 optimal on CNN-DM). On MT-Bench with EAGLE at T=0.1 and 2 drafts: Greedy 84.7% acceptance, 1.13× speedup, vs 79.4% and 1.04× for RRS without replacement. Stated: greedy is worse at temperature 1, so not uniformly best; default experiments at T=0.7 with 3 drafts — [Hu et al. 2025](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18779)
- "Multi-Draft Speculative Sampling: Canonical Decomposition and Theoretical Limits" (Khisti et al., Qualcomm/Toronto, arXiv:2410.18234, v2 May 2025; listed as ICLR 2025 in its PDF header): the optimal rule decomposes into importance-weighted sampling of one candidate, then single-draft speculative sampling. Gives a necessary and sufficient condition for acceptance probability 1 (sum of q over any subset S at least the square of sum of p over S) and a closed form for K=2 identical drafts. Assumes i.i.d. drafts from one model; Remark 1 says it extends to joint/different draft distributions. Stated limitations: closed form only for two identical drafts; >2 drafts only numerical evidence; computing optimal weights is an LP; faster heuristics lose acceptance — [Khisti et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18234v2); [ICLR PDF listing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18234)
- "Global Resolution: Optimal Multi-Draft Speculative Sampling via Convex Minimization" (Thomas and Pal, Ritual/Stanford, arXiv:2511.15898, 19 Nov 2025; ICLR 2026 proceedings). Solves the OTLP via canonical decomposition, max-flow reduction, polymatroid closed forms when ψ is submodular, and convex solvers. Reports "90% acceptance and under 100 ms of overhead per generated token" at T=1. Llama-3 at 100 ms/token: General LP 83.94%, max-flow 89.01%, Global Resolution (τ=10⁻³) 90.04%. Integrated into SpecTr it improves "walltime decoding by nearly 2×" over the baseline. Approximate version deviates from target marginal by at most 15τ in L1, so exactness is approximate. Main setting is i.i.d. drafts at one position; independent drafts from n different distributions in Appendix C. No T=0 results. Stated limitations: variable convex-step runtime with low success rates in some settings (14%-23%), exponential number of inner residuals, reliance on submodularity, top-k vocabulary truncation, non-i.i.d. drafts and multi-step evaluation left to future work — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15898v1); [ICLR 2026 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/83995c2acef585ae0f0d647154cfdd85-Paper-Conference.pdf); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=gpsczXOsHn)

Single chain, multi-step (sequence-level instead of token-level):
- "Block Verification Accelerates Speculative Decoding" (Sun, Mendlovic, Leviathan, Aharoni, Beirami, Ro, Suresh, Google Research, arXiv:2403.10444; ICLR 2025 per mlanthology). Computes running weights p_i along the drafted path, considers every sub-block, accepts the longest accepted sub-block, residual from a backward pass. Needs only conditionals along the sampled path. Proven lossless and optimal in expected tokens per iteration among verifiers with that partial information; never worse than token verification. Reports 7%-10% block efficiency and 5%-8% wall-clock gains over token verification. Toy example: 10/9 (token) vs 11/9 (block) vs 12/9 (full-information ideal). Does not treat trees or multiple drafts [partial text] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10444v2); [ICLR 2025 listing](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2025/sun2025iclr-block/)
- "Overcoming Joint Intractability with Lossless Hierarchical Speculative Decoding" (HSD; Zhou, Huang, Li, Wu, Wang, Zhang, Lin (Qwen/Alibaba), Cheng (UW), arXiv:2601.05724, Jan 2026). Scans backward from the end of the draft over "accessible branches" (prefixes where full next-token distributions are available), accepts the longest prefix, resamples once with "capped" branch divergences that redistribute excess mass from over-covered branches. Naive version needs γ−τ+1 extra target calls; capped version needs one resampling step. Lossless proofs in Appendix B/C (the general proof was partly truncated in what I could read) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05724v2); [code](https://github.com/ZhouYuxuanYX/Hierarchical-Speculative-Decoding)
- HSD results at T=1 (Qwen2.5, 0.5B draft, GPTQ 8-bit, single H20): GSM8K block efficiency +5.2% (14B), +5.4% (32B), +3.3% (72B) over tokenwise; Blockwise only +2.3% at 14B. Averaged across datasets about 6.2% BE and 6.7% decoding speed over tokenwise and blockwise. Multi-draft (11 drafts, baseline RRS with replacement): about 5.9% BE and 4.7% speed. Llama-3.1 70B: BE 7.43 vs 6.83 (+8.8%). EAGLE-3H: BE 3.55 vs 3.40 (+4.4%), speed +12.4%. Temperatures 0.6/0.8/1.0 in ablation; no T=0 results. HSD needs computable q for the branches; with EAGLE-3 top-K drafting the draft probabilities equal 1 and "verification degenerates"; authors attribute part of the EAGLE-3 gain to noise/float effects and the speedup to avoiding loops. Tree attention not evaluated; Blockwise left out of multi-draft comparisons as hard to extend — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05724v2)

Trees (multi-step and multi-draft together):
- "Traversal Verification for Speculative Tree Decoding" (Weng, Hu, Chen, Liu, Mei, Qiu, Tian, Shi; arXiv:2505.12398, May 2025, v2 Nov 2025; NeurIPS 2025 poster). Each node gets a sequence-level acceptance p(X_i) = min(p(X_{i−1})·Mb/Ms, 1). Verification starts at the deepest leaf of the first chain; on rejection the leaf is removed and the parent's target residual becomes norm([p·Mb − Ms]+) over its children, draft distribution renormalised without the rejected child (without replacement), parent acceptance becomes p′ = Σ[p·Mb − Ms]+ / (Σ[p·Mb − Ms]+ + 1 − p). Proven lossless; equals Block Verification on a chain and claimed an optimal valid chain verifier; no optimality claim for general trees. Children may be drawn with or without replacement; baseline is RRS without replacement (RRSw) as in EAGLE — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.12398); [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/119595)
- Traversal Verification results at T=1: Llama3.2-1B→Llama3.1-8B acceptance length chain 3.88→3.99 (+2.8%), binary tree 4.63→4.73 (+2.2%), EAGLE sparse tree 4.49→4.60 (+2.4%); throughput +2.5%/+1.7%/+2.1%. Llama-68M→Llama2-7B: chain +5.7%, binary +4.9%, EAGLE sparse +3.8%. Gains grow with temperature: at T=0.2 +1.0%/+1.2%/+1.5%, at T=0.8 +2.2%/+1.7%/+1.7%. At T=0 no gain (method applies only to stochastic decoding). Stated limitations: traversal overhead lowers throughput relative to acceptance gain; pruning low-probability subsequences suggested but not evaluated; one GPU type (RTX A6000) and two model pairs; MT-Bench LLM-judge quality check only supporting evidence — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.12398)
- "UniVer: A Unified Perspective for Multi-step and Multi-draft Speculative Decoding" (Weng, Hu, Yairi; arXiv:2605.04543, May 2026). Treats tree verification as conditional OT with prefix acceptance probabilities as scaling factors; top-down allocation phase plus post-order decision phase. Lossless via "locally lossless" tree (Thm 3) plus a two-stage-verifier lemma (Thm 4). Conditional optimality for a single-layer subtree given prefix probability (Thm 5), not global; expected length at least that of Greedy (Thm 6). Assumes children drawn by mixed strategy: top m−1 deterministic plus one sampled from the residual. At T=1, Vicuna-7B binary tree depth 5: τ 3.29 vs 3.06 RRSw (+7.5%), Greedy 3.21, Traversal +2.3%. Gains over RRSw 7.5% (2×5), 4.8% (3×3), 4.2% (EAGLE tree); abstract range 4.2%-8.5% (8.5% on Llama3.1-8B, 2.95 vs 2.72). At T=0.3 +1.5%. Stated: "UniVer possesses no extra performance gain under or near temperature = 0"; gains depend on topology and sampling strategy; co-design of sampling, verification and tree structure left open — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04543)
- "Layer Verification Accelerates Speculative Tree Decoding" (Cha, Cho, Yun; ICML 2026 AdaptFM workshop; arXiv id not on the page). Extends any single-step verifier to trees, scoring nodes with the single-step rule, balancing acceptance across each layer, sweeping layer by layer, then selecting the accepted endpoint and sampling a corrected token. Proven lossless when the base verifier is lossless; with Global Resolution as base it reaches near-optimal expected acceptance length for both single-step multi-candidate and multi-step single-candidate drafts. No numbers on the page [abstract only] — [project page](https://chulheeyun.github.io/publication/cha2026layer/)

Block/diffusion draft trees (independent per-position marginals):
- DDTree, "Accelerating Speculative Decoding with Block Diffusion Draft Trees" (Ringel and Romano, Technion; arXiv:2604.12989, Apr 2026). One DFlash pass gives marginals q_i per position, "a marginal distribution for position i, not a path-conditioned distribution". Prefix score = product of per-position probabilities. Proves the B highest-probability prefixes form the optimal prefix-closed tree for expected acceptance under this factorised surrogate; best-first heap over top-K per depth, O(B log B). Verification: one tree-attention pass, then a walk following the target's own decoding rule (greedy or temperature sampling), first unmatched target token becomes the bonus. Stated limitations: optimality is for the surrogate not the target (Remark 1); independence across block positions assumed; probabilities assumed in (0,1); tree attention needs PyTorch SDPA (no FlashAttention-2 support); best budget shifts across hardware; depends on DFlash drafters; three targets only; sampling-mode losslessness of the walk not separately analysed — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12989); [PDF](https://liranringel.github.io/ddtree/DDTree.pdf)
- DDTree vs DFlash (speedup / τ), Qwen3-8B: MATH-500 T=0 5.56×/7.79 → 7.52×/10.73; T=1 4.56×/6.46 → 6.59×/9.54. Alpaca T=0 2.07×/3.12 → 3.36×/5.09; T=1 1.95×/2.94 → 3.20×/4.89. Qwen3-Coder-30B HumanEval T=0 6.09×/8.02 → 8.22×/10.72; T=1 5.64×/7.60 → 7.88×/10.42. Improves all 60 entries; gains larger at T=0. Speedup peaks around budget 256-512 on MATH-500/Qwen3-8B while τ keeps rising. 8×H200, single-sequence decoding. (One table header says temperature "1.6", the text says 1.0) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12989)
- BASTION (Oh, Cao, Kim, Jung, Ahmad, Bae, Yun; KAIST/Samsung; arXiv:2605.29727, 2026): best-first expansion over per-position top-K with path score = product of drafter probabilities (proved optimal for the surrogate at each node count), plus a roofline latency-model controller that stops at the first budget where estimated speedup drops. Acceptance described as longest root-to-leaf match against the target's greedy top-1; abstract claims distribution preservation but the text does not explain T>0 handling. A100, avg of 8 benchmarks: Qwen3-8B T=0 6.61× / τ 8.48 (EAGLE-3 2.70×/2.32); T=1 6.53×/8.38 vs DFlash 3.96×/5.26 and EAGLE-3 2.50×/2.16. Stated limitations: batch size 1 only; runtime-drift deployments open; online EMA fallback fragile — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29727v1)
- CaDDTree, "Cost-Aware Diffusion Draft Trees" (Zhang, Qiu, He, Dai; arXiv:2606.01813, 1 Jun 2026): DDTree construction, but picks node count n per round by maximising (1 + Φ(T)) / (C_d + C_v(|T|; ℓ)) with offline-profiled, context-length-dependent verification cost. T=0 Qwen3-4B MATH-500 4.53 ms/tok vs AR 30.65 and DFlash 5.93 (my ratio ~6.8× and ~1.31×); roughly equal to a DDTree-oracle budget. T=1 results in an appendix I could not read. Stated: independence "can accelerate error accumulation"; offline profiling; Qwen3-4B/8B only — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01813)
- PRESTO (Wang, Ye, Cheng, Fu, Wang, Zhu, Zhao, Kautz, Molchanov, Shi, Zhang; arXiv:2607.22634, 2026): adds a prefix-dependent 3-gram term, score log q_d(t) + λ·log ρ_d(t | c_d), λ=0.2, beam search (width 10, top-k 10). Leaves verification unchanged (prefix-based, left to right); losslessness under sampling not stated. Batch size 1, B200. dFlash T=0: τ ~7.9→10.9 (MATH-500), ~1.5× over dFlash; T=1: 168→258 tok/s (Qwen3-4B, 1.54×). Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion quadratic mode T=1: 1.4×→3.0×, τ 2.4→4.7. Stated: n-gram signal is short-range only; single-request only (no larger batch, no vLLM for dFlash); speedups decline when verification becomes compute-bound, budgets capped at 512 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22634)
- DARTree (Li, Luo, Shang, Shen; arXiv:2608.13524, 13 Aug 2026): restores intra-block dependence with a causal correction head carrying recurrent state along each path, fixed-width depth-wise supertree then top-B pruning. Verification "inherited unchanged from prior work"; abstract says lossless. Qwen3-4B T=0 GSM8K: DFlash 5.24×/6.53, DDTree 6.62×/8.47, DARTree 9.73×/12.97; overall avg DDTree 5.79×/7.78 vs DARTree 6.99×/9.81. T=1 results not in what I read. Noted approximations: fixed-width schedule approximates global best-first; heap/top-B equivalence only for depth bonus β ≤ 0 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13524v1)

### Inferences
- The block-diffusion tree papers build deterministic trees (top-B prefixes). For a deterministic candidate set the draft "probability" is 1, so q-based rejection rules (RRS, Traversal, UniVer, HSD) have nothing to reweight; HSD itself notes this degeneration for EAGLE-3 top-K drafting. With deterministic children, the lossless rule they use (sample the target token, follow it if it is a child) accepts each child with probability p(child), which is already the maximum for that fixed set. So for DDTree-style trees the verification rule is not where losses are; gains require stochastic drafting from the marginals (sampling children rather than taking top-K), which is the setting Tree-HSD-style rules need. This is my inference from the cited setups, not a stated result.
- The "drafter's marginals are independent" structure has only been used as a surrogate for tree selection, never as an assumption inside a verifier. A lossless verifier for children sampled from product-of-marginals proposals, and its optimal acceptance, appears to be open.
- Overlap with Ege's Tree-HSD (reach-reweighted recursive rejection sampling over draft trees): Traversal Verification's residual norm([p·Mb − Ms]+) weights the parent's target mass by the path acceptance probability p before subtracting the draft, which is close to a reach-reweighting of RRS. UniVer explicitly uses "prefix acceptance probabilities as scaling factors" in a conditional-OT allocation, and Layer Verification balances acceptance per layer. These three are the closest prior work and should be compared head-to-head. Their reported gains on real LLMs (2.2%-8.5% over RRSw) are smaller than Tree-HSD's +12-18% in simulation, but the baselines differ (RRS without replacement vs SpecInfer-style) and simulation vs LLM settings differ, so the numbers are not directly comparable.

### Gaps
- Exact Block Verification formulas (h_i, residual) and per-temperature numbers not visible in the text I read.
- Layer Verification arXiv id and numbers not found.
- DARTree, CaDDTree T=1 numbers not read (appendix / truncated).
- No paper found that derives optimal or lossless acceptance specifically for conditionally independent (block/diffusion) proposals.
- Whether DDTree/BASTION/PRESTO's T>0 walk is exactly "sample target token and match a child" is inferred from their descriptions, not quoted from code.

## Q2. Is there lossless verification with an intermediate (cheap/quantized) verifier inside a hierarchy under sampling? Gains at T>0?

### Takeaway
Yes, several three-level schemes exist (HiSpec, Polybasic, ML-SpecQD, QuantSpec-style self-speculation), but the lossless-under-sampling arguments are mostly sketches, and T>0 numbers are sparse. HiSpec is the only one I found with an explicit two-stage rejection rule and a sampled-mode speedup (1.54× vs 2.00× greedy). Note the name clash: HSD (arXiv:2601.05724) is a verification rule over a single draft/target pair, not a model hierarchy.

### Cited Findings
- HiSpec (Kumar, Sanghavi, Das; UT Austin; arXiv:2510.01336, Oct 2025): early-exit layer L_i (about 1/4 depth) verifies drafts from L_d (about 1/8 depth); then the full model verifies. Rule: accept S_k if u < min(1, p_{L_i}/p_{L_d}), residual max(0, p_{L_i} − p_{L_d}); then accept V_k if u < min(1, p_{L_f}/p_{L_i}), bonus from max(0, p_{L_f} − p_{L_i}). Losslessness only argued in Appendix G as "a direct application of the standard speculative-decoding correctness proof"; no empirical distribution test. Llama3-8B ShareGPT: 2.01× (greedy default); Table 8 config 2.00× greedy vs 1.54× with rejection sampling. Stated: sampling slower because of full distributions; layer placement varies by model; few exit layers in post-trained early-exit models; multiple intermediate verifiers, trees and EAGLE/Medusa integration left to future work — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01336v2)
- Polybasic Speculative Decoding (Wang, Li, Ma, Zheng, Chao, Xiao, Ji; ICML 2025; arXiv:2510.26527). Draft M_n proposes K tokens, verified by M_{n−1}, ..., M_1; three-model algorithm with M_2 pre-verifying until a count threshold. Lemma 3.1 gives inference time T = Σ(N/L_i)T_i + β(N/L_{n−1})T_n; Theorem 3.2 says inserting a model helps if its cost is small relative to the acceptance gain; Theorem 3.3 a variance formula. Claims distribution preservation in the abstract but gives no formal proof of losslessness for the chain. Speedups (temperature not stated): Vicuna-7B 3.48×, LLaMA3-8B-Instruct 3.31×, LLaMA2-Chat-7B 3.66×, Qwen2-7B 3.28×. Stated limitations: 4+ models hard without training; smaller gains with long context and larger models (Vicuna-13B 2.69×, LLaMA-70B 2.92×); partly sketched proofs; mostly batch size 1 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26527v1); [ICML 2025](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2025/wang2025icml-polybasic)
- ML-SpecQD (arXiv:2503.13565) and "Speculative Decoding Meets Quantization" (arXiv:2505.22179) appeared in search as multi-level quantized-draft hierarchies; I did not read their full texts — [ML-SpecQD](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13565v1); [SD meets quantization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22179)

### Inferences
- Under sampling, a two-stage chain of standard rejection tests (HiSpec style) is lossless because each stage is a valid speculative sampler for its own target, but acceptance at the final stage is measured against the intermediate distribution, not the drafter, so a cheap intermediate verifier that is far from the target can lower final acceptance. None of the papers I read quantifies this at T>0.
- None of the hierarchy papers combines an intermediate verifier with tree drafts or with sequence-level (block/Traversal/HSD) rules. HiSpec lists trees as future work.

### Gaps
- QuantSpec, ML-SpecQD, cascade/staged SD T>0 numbers not verified in this pass.
- No paper found proving losslessness of an intermediate verifier operating on trees.

## Q3. Tree construction from parallel drafts under a node budget: batch size 1 vs larger batch

### Takeaway
Every block-diffusion tree paper (DDTree, BASTION, CaDDTree, PRESTO, DARTree) evaluates at batch size 1 / single request. Budget selection is done by latency models (BASTION roofline, CaDDTree context-length-aware profiling) or a fixed cap (PRESTO 512). Larger batches are explicitly named as untreated.

### Cited Findings
- DDTree: optimal tree for the factorised surrogate = top-B prefixes; speedup peaks at budgets 256-512 while τ keeps rising; best budget shifts across hardware — [DDTree](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12989)
- BASTION: roofline-style verification-latency model calibrated by a linear fit per GPU and target; "evaluation targets batch size 1"; larger batches "change the verification-cost profile and need separate treatment" — [BASTION](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29727v1)
- CaDDTree: chooses tree size per round from (1 + Φ(T)) / (C_d + C_v(|T|; ℓ)); verification cost depends on context length ℓ; no batch-size discussion — [CaDDTree](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01813)
- PRESTO: single-request only; "speedups decline once verification becomes compute-bound at large budgets", budgets capped at 512 — [PRESTO](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22634)
- Speed-of-Light (Pankratov and Alistarh): with a deterministic drafter, the optimal draft for verifier capacity P is the P highest path-probability nodes (prefix-closed tree, Lemma 1), matching EAGLE-2-style selection without depth/width caps — [arXiv:2512.11718](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11718)

### Inferences
- Since all evaluations are at batch 1 where extra verifier width is nearly free up to a few hundred tokens, the reported tree gains over DFlash (e.g. ~1.39× BASTION, ~1.5× PRESTO) may not carry to batched serving. No paper measures this.

### Gaps
- No paper found on tree construction from parallel drafts at batch > 1.

## Q4. Theoretical results: acceptance bounds, error correlation between branches, value of draft diversity

### Takeaway
There is an explicit upper bound on accepted tokens as a function of verifier parallelism and target entropy (Speed-of-Light, ~log(P)/µ), exact OT-based optimal single-step multi-draft acceptance, and results that sampling without replacement (a form of diversity) beats with replacement. I found no result linking per-position accuracy of an independent-marginal drafter to acceptance length, or modeling error correlation between branches.

### Cited Findings
- "Speculative Decoding Speed-of-Light: Optimal Lower Bounds via Branching Random Walks" (Pankratov, Alistarh; arXiv:2512.11718, 12 Dec 2025). For a deterministic drafter, E[X] ≤ ((µ + µ(2))/µ²)·log(P) + O(1), with µ the expected target entropy and µ(2) the second log-moment; as P→∞, E[X] ≈ log(P)/µ. Measured µ at T=1: Llama 3.1 8B 0.683, Llama 3.3 70B 0.153, R1-Distill-Llama-8B 0.554, Qwen3 8B 0.319. EAGLE-3 is about 2× short of the bound at P=60. Stated limitations: i.i.d. acceptance assumption ("unreasonable" for language), constant verifier latency, drafter overhead ignored, perfect-knowledge drafter, deterministic drafting only, error terms not explicit — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11718)
- Optimal single-step multi-draft acceptance α* = 1 + min_H [P(H) − Q(H)], without-replacement better than with-replacement; gap between verifiers and bound grows with temperature and draft count — [Hu et al. 2025](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18779); polymatroid/submodular structure and fast computation — [Global Resolution](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15898v1)
- Block Verification is optimal among valid chain verifiers with path-only information; Traversal equals it on chains (Thm 3.4) — [Block Verification](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10444v2); [Traversal](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.12398)
- UniVer: conditional optimality per single-layer subtree, not global — [UniVer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04543)
- "When Is a Draft Accepted? A Theory of Acceptance in Speculative Decoding" (arXiv:2606.30265, 2026): rejection regions of common criteria are lower level sets of the target; exact KL needed for rejection; margin-based bounds for greedy, relaxed (additive, multiplicative, top-m) and entropy-thresholded acceptance; certificates for greedy tree decoding when the target's greedy token is in the drafter's top-m. Targets deterministic local acceptance events, framed as complementing distribution-preserving analyses [abstract only] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30265)

### Inferences
- Speed-of-Light's Lemma 1 (top-P path-probability nodes optimal) is the same structure as DDTree's top-B prefix result, but DDTree applies it to the drafter's surrogate, not the target. The gap between surrogate-optimal and target-optimal trees is exactly what independence errors cause; no paper bounds it.

### Gaps
- No found theory on correlation of errors between sibling branches, or on acceptance length given per-position marginal accuracy of a parallel drafter.

## Q5. Lossy / typical acceptance (for completeness) and stated open problems

### Takeaway
Lossy rules continue in 2026 (margin-based per-step promotion), mostly evaluated at greedy decoding. Open problems stated across papers converge on: non-i.i.d./structured drafts, global tree optimality, co-design of sampling/verification/tree, T>0 behavior of diffusion trees, and batch > 1.

### Cited Findings
- "Margins, Not Windows" (Urbán, Kwon, Venieris, Mascolo; arXiv:2609.02897, 2026): at first mismatch, promote draft token if p_target(draft)/p_target(top1) ≥ κ. EAGLE-3, SGLang, A100, batch 1, greedy. Llama-3.1-8B GSM8K 2.28× / 93% accuracy recovery vs FLy 2.17× / 95%; with dynamic tree 2.44× average at 98% recovery; 17-56% faster than EAGLE-3. Recovery drops to ~82% in some cells; κ tuned per model/benchmark — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02897v1)
