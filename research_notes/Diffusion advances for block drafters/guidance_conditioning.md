# Guidance, conditioning and representation alignment in diffusion models (2025-2026), and transfer to a block-diffusion speculative drafter

Setting for the transfer ideas: a DFlash-style drafter (about 1B params, 5 layers) drafts a 16-token block in parallel, conditioned on Qwen3-8B hidden features at verified positions (injected as K/V in every drafter layer). The drafter is context-limited because it lacks the target's computation over its own drafted tokens. A cheap estimator (Qwen3-0.6B plus a learned map) produces noisy estimates of target hidden states over drafted tokens. Prior findings: training on noisy (low-bit) target features recovers much more than training with missing features; features computed after a wrong token are useless.

Verification note: items marked [verified] had their abstract or paper page fetched in this session. Items marked [background, not re-verified] are cited from prior knowledge with the arXiv link; their numbers should be spot-checked before going into a paper.

## Q1. CFG variants and successors, guidance in discrete/masked diffusion LMs, and whether a small model's estimate can act as guidance or a contrast signal

### Takeaway
The strongest 2024-2026 result is that guiding a model with a *weaker version of itself* (autoguidance) beats classic CFG by a wide margin (EDM2-S ImageNet-512 FID 2.23 with CFG vs 1.34 with autoguidance), and that the guidance effect can be distilled into training so inference needs one pass (Model-guidance FID 1.34; GFT matches CFG at half the sampling cost). For discrete LMs, guidance works best when the "weak" branch is built locally from low-confidence tokens (A-CFG, +3.9 GPQA over CFG). For the drafter, the most promising transfer is a training-time, single-pass version of "strong vs weak conditioning" contrast, not test-time two-pass guidance, since drafting latency is the bottleneck.

### Cited Findings
**Classic CFG and direct variants (background)**
- CFG mixes conditional and unconditional predictions of one jointly trained model (condition dropped with some probability during training), trading diversity for fidelity; costs two forward passes per step. [background] — [Ho & Salimans, arXiv:2207.12598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598)
- Guidance interval: applying CFG only in a limited middle range of noise levels (disabling it at high and low noise) improved the ImageNet-512 record FID from 1.81 to 1.40 (EDM2-XXL). [background, not re-verified] — [Kynkäänniemi et al., arXiv:2404.07724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07724). Consistent with the EDM2-S numbers reproduced in a 2025 paper: CFG 2.23 vs guidance interval 1.68 [verified] — [In-situ Autoguidance, arXiv:2510.17136](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17136v1)
- CFG++ (ICLR 2025): reframes CFG as an off-manifold problem and uses the unconditional noise for the renoising step with a small guidance scale in [0,1], improving sample quality and DDIM inversion at low scales. [background, not re-verified] — [Chung et al., arXiv:2406.08070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08070)
- APG (Adaptive Projected Guidance, ICLR 2025): decomposes the CFG update into components parallel and orthogonal to the conditional prediction, down-weights the parallel part (the source of oversaturation), plus rescaling and a momentum term; allows high guidance scales without oversaturation. [background, not re-verified] — [Sadat et al., arXiv:2410.02416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02416)

**Guidance from a weaker/bad version of the model**
- Autoguidance (NeurIPS 2024): guide with a smaller and/or less-trained version of the same model instead of an unconditional model; the improvement comes from the weak model making the *same kind of errors more strongly*, so extrapolating away from it removes them without collapsing diversity. Reported record FIDs 1.01 (ImageNet-64) and 1.25 (ImageNet-512). [background, not re-verified] — [Karras et al., arXiv:2406.02507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02507)
- Same-setup comparison on EDM2-S ImageNet-512: baseline FID 2.56, +CFG 2.23, +guidance interval 1.68, +autoguidance 1.34; FD-DINOv2 68.64 / 52.32 / 46.25 / 36.67. [verified] — [arXiv:2510.17136](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17136v1)
- In-situ Autoguidance (2025): builds the "bad" model from the same network with dropout on (p=0.1), guidance D_good + w(D_good - D_bad), w=2. It does *not* help: FID 2.57 vs 2.56 baseline, FD-DINOv2 worse (90.05 vs 68.64). Authors frame it as a zero-cost proof of concept. [verified] — [arXiv:2510.17136](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17136v1)
- Other 2025 training-free self-guidance variants exist (e.g., stochastic sub-network guidance S^2-Guidance; spatiotemporal skip guidance for video, which degrades the model by skipping layers). [titles only; not fetched] — [S^2-Guidance, arXiv:2508.12880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12880); [STG, arXiv:2411.18664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18664)

**Guidance folded into training (CFG-free)**
- Model-guidance (MG, 2025): adds the posterior of the condition to the training objective, using the model as an implicit classifier, so no CFG at sampling. ImageNet-256 FID without CFG: DiT-XL/2 9.62 -> 2.03, SiT-XL/2 8.61 -> 1.34 (SiT-XL/2 with CFG: 2.06). Reports >=6.5x convergence speedup (6.5x DiT-B/2, 7x SiT-B/2), one forward pass per step (about 2x inference speed), and comparable FID to LightningDiT at about 12% of sampling compute. ImageNet-512: SiT-XL/2 2.62 -> 2.24. [verified] — [Tang et al., arXiv:2502.12154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12154)
- Guidance-Free Training (GFT, ICML 2025): parameterizes the conditional output as a linear interpolation of a sampling network and a stop-gradient unconditional network, with pseudo-temperature beta = 1/(1+s) as an input. Same ML objective as CFG, 10-20% extra training time, about 50% sampling cost cut. FID: DiT-XL/2 2.11 (CFG) vs 1.99 (GFT, fine-tuned); VAR-d30 1.92 vs 1.91; LlamaGen-L from scratch 3.06 vs 2.52; MAR-B slightly worse 2.27 vs 2.39. Fine-tuning needs 1-5% of pretraining epochs. Applies to diffusion, AR and masked-prediction models. [verified] — [Chen et al., arXiv:2501.15420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15420)

**Guidance in discrete / masked diffusion LMs**
- A-CFG (NeurIPS 2025): instead of a fixed fully-masked unconditional input, at each step it re-masks the tokens where the model currently has low confidence to build a localized "unconditional" input, focusing guidance on ambiguous positions. Integrated into a masked diffusion LM (LLaDA-class); +3.9 points on GPQA over standard CFG, consistent gains across benchmarks. [verified] — [Li et al., arXiv:2505.20199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20199)
- Simple guidance for discrete diffusion (ICLR 2025): derives discrete CFG and classifier guidance for masked and uniform-noise discrete diffusion (introduces UDLM). [background, not re-verified] — [Schiff et al., arXiv:2412.10193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10193); see also discrete guidance for CTMC models [background] — [Nisonoff et al., arXiv:2406.01572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01572)
- LM analog of autoguidance: contrastive decoding scores tokens by expert log-prob minus amateur (small model) log-prob under a plausibility constraint. [background] — [Li et al., arXiv:2210.15097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15097)

### Inferences
- Two-pass test-time guidance (CFG, autoguidance) doubles drafter cost per denoising step. For a drafter whose value is low latency, the transferable form is the training-time version: MG/GFT show the guided distribution can be learned into a single network with modest overhead.
- Concrete idea A (autoguidance-style contrast in conditioning space, distilled): define two conditioning regimes for the same drafter: "strong" = true target hidden states over drafted positions (available at training time from the target forward pass on gold or self-drafted continuations), "weak" = the 0.6B estimator's features (or no features). Train the drafter on weak conditioning but with a GFT/MG-style target: logits_weak + w(logits_strong - logits_weak) built from a stop-grad pass with strong features. At inference only the weak path runs. This is autoguidance where "bad model" = "same drafter with worse context", and the gap is exactly the missing target computation.
- Concrete idea B (logit-space contrast at test time, only if cheap): draft logits = l(est. features) + w(l(est. features) - l(no features)). The "no features" pass is cheap if the drafter shares K/V for verified positions; whether w>0 helps is an empirical question. The In-situ result is a warning: a weak branch that differs by random noise (dropout) gives no signal; the weak branch must differ in a *structured* way (less capacity, less context), as in true autoguidance.
- Concrete idea C (A-CFG analog): for positions inside the 16-token block where the drafter is low-confidence, re-mask them and/or drop their estimated features to form the contrast input. This localizes guidance to the tokens most likely to be rejected, matching acceptance-length economics (one early rejection truncates the block).
- Guidance changes the drafted distribution away from the drafter's own; in speculative decoding with sampling, acceptance depends on matching the target distribution, so guidance should be tuned on acceptance length, not on any sample-quality proxy. Guidance-interval logic suggests applying contrast only at certain denoising steps (e.g., late steps when tokens are being committed).

### Gaps
- No paper found that applies autoguidance or CFG to a speculative drafter, or uses a weak model's hidden-state estimates as a guidance contrast. Searches for diffusion LM guidance did not surface such work.
- APG, CFG++ and guidance-interval numbers above were not re-fetched this session.
- No quantitative evidence found on whether guidance improves *calibration* to a target distribution (what speculative sampling needs) rather than fidelity.

## Q2. Predictor-corrector samplers and self-conditioning; transfer to iterating a drafter with an external corrector

### Takeaway
Self-conditioning (feeding the model its own previous clean-data estimate) is a cheap, well-established gain in continuous and embedding diffusion; for masked discrete diffusion, 2025 work shows remasking/corrector steps fix the "locked-in token" problem (ReMDM: MAUVE 0.656 vs 0.035 for MDLM at T=4096; gains on LLaDA-8B Countdown 46.1 vs 45.2), and learned per-token quality heads (PRISM) can choose what to revise in the same forward pass. For the drafter, the natural mapping is: drafter pass = predictor, the 0.6B estimator pass over the drafted block = corrector-side information, with self-conditioning on the previous draft.

### Cited Findings
**Self-conditioning (background)**
- Analog Bits introduced self-conditioning: the denoiser receives its own previous estimate of x0 as an extra input; trained by computing that estimate with stop-gradient on 50% of steps (else zeros), adding little training cost and giving substantial sample-quality gains. [background, not re-verified] — [Chen et al., arXiv:2208.04202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04202)
- CDCD (continuous diffusion on token embeddings) uses self-conditioning together with score interpolation and time warping for language. [background, not re-verified] — [Dieleman et al., arXiv:2211.15089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15089)

**Predictor-corrector for discrete diffusion**
- CTMC discrete diffusion introduced predictor-corrector sampling for discrete state spaces. [background] — [Campbell et al., arXiv:2205.14987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14987); informed correctors for discrete diffusion [background, title-level] — [Zhao et al., arXiv:2407.21243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21243)
- ReMDM (NeurIPS 2025): adds a remasking probability sigma_t so decoded tokens can be remasked and regenerated; sigma_t = 0 recovers MDLM, so it applies to pretrained checkpoints without retraining. Theorem: its posterior equals an MDLM predictor step followed by a marginal-preserving corrector; forward-backward and DFM correctors are special cases. Results: OpenWebText MAUVE 0.403/0.610/0.656 at T=1024/2048/4096 vs MDLM 0.042-0.035 and best corrector baseline 0.294 (T=2048); at T=512, 0.350 vs 0.211 (MDLM+DFM); at T=128 slightly worse than MDLM+FB (0.057 vs 0.064). ImageNet T=64 FID 4.45 vs MaskGiT 4.85. LLaDA-8B-Instruct Countdown pass@1 46.1 vs 45.2 (LLaDA) vs 44.8 (LLaDA-DFM). [verified] — [Wang et al., arXiv:2503.00307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00307v4)
- PRISM (ICLR 2026; name collides with Ege's own PRISM paper): lightweight fine-tuning of any pretrained MDM with a self-correction loss that provably learns per-token quality scores in the same forward pass, used to pick low-quality tokens to remask; no RL or verifier. Evaluated on Sudoku, unconditional text (170M) and code with LLaDA-8B; abstract gives no numbers. [verified] — [arXiv:2510.01384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01384v3)
- Further 2025-2026 remasking/self-correction work exists (RIV recursive introspection; self-reflective remasking; generalized discrete diffusion with self-correction). [titles only; not fetched] — [arXiv:2509.23625](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23625v1); [arXiv:2509.23653](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23653v1); [arXiv:2603.02230](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02230)

### Inferences
- Concrete idea D (self-conditioned two-pass drafting with an external corrector signal): pass 1: drafter proposes 16 tokens from verified-position target features. Corrector: run Qwen3-0.6B + map over the drafted block to get estimated target hidden states. Pass 2: drafter re-drafts with (a) its own pass-1 logits/embeddings as self-conditioning input and (b) estimated features as extra K/V. Train with Analog-Bits-style 50% stop-grad self-conditioning so pass 2 is optional at inference (graceful fallback to one pass).
- Your finding "features after a wrong token are useless" maps directly onto the corrector design: the second pass should only trust estimated features up to the first low-confidence position. A PRISM-style per-token quality head on the drafter could predict where features become unreliable and gate them (zero/mask K/V beyond that point), and doubles as an early-truncation signal for the draft length.
- ReMDM shows correctors pay off mostly with more steps (T >= L), and can lose at very few steps (T=128 case). For a drafter with 1-2 steps, expect smaller gains; the correction budget should be compared against simply drafting fewer tokens.
- ReMDM-style remasking works on pretrained checkpoints with no retraining; a cheap first experiment is remasking the drafter's lowest-confidence tokens and redrafting them conditioned on estimated features, without new training.

### Gaps
- No found work combines a diffusion drafter with an external (different-model) corrector; ReMDM and PRISM use the same model as corrector.
- Exact self-conditioning gains in Analog Bits/CDCD were not re-verified this session.
- Quantitative PRISM results were not available from the abstract.

## Q3. Representation alignment (REPA, REPA-E, VA-VAE, REG, 2025-2026 follow-ups); can the drafter align its internal states to target (or estimator) hidden states?

### Takeaway
Aligning intermediate denoiser features to a strong frozen encoder is one of the largest training-efficiency levers of 2024-2026 (REPA 17.5x faster; REPA-E 17x over REPA and 45x over vanilla, FID 1.26; REG 63x over SiT-XL/2 and 23x over REPA by injecting just one encoder token). 2025 analyses show the *structure* of the target (pairwise similarity between tokens) matters more than its global quality (iREPA, 27 encoders). This directly suggests an auxiliary loss aligning drafter hidden states over drafted positions to Qwen3-8B hidden states at those positions, which forces the drafter to internalize part of the missing target computation at zero inference cost.

### Cited Findings
- REPA (ICLR 2025): regularizes early DiT/SiT hidden states (via an MLP projection) to match patch features of a frozen self-supervised encoder (DINOv2) on the clean image; over 17.5x faster training of SiT-XL and FID 1.42 with CFG + guidance interval. [background, not re-verified] — [Yu et al., arXiv:2410.06940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06940)
- REPA-E (ICCV 2025): end-to-end training of VAE + latent diffusion fails with diffusion loss alone but works with the REPA loss; >17x faster than REPA and 45x faster than vanilla; ImageNet-256 FID 1.26 with CFG and 1.83 without; the end-to-end process also improves the VAE latent space. [verified] — [Leng et al., arXiv:2504.10483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10483)
- VA-VAE: aligns the VAE latent space with a vision foundation model to resolve the reconstruction-vs-generation tradeoff; LightningDiT reaches FID 1.35 on ImageNet-256 and about 21x faster convergence (FID 2.11 in 64 epochs). [background, not re-verified] — [Yao et al., arXiv:2501.01423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01423)
- REG (NeurIPS 2025 oral): instead of an alignment loss, concatenates one high-level class token from a pretrained foundation model to the noisy latents and denoises image latents and the token jointly from noise; <0.5% FLOPs/latency overhead. SiT-XL/2+REG trains 63x faster than SiT-XL/2 and 23x faster than SiT-XL/2+REPA; SiT-L/2+REG at 400K iterations beats SiT-XL/2+REPA at 4M. [verified] — [Wu et al., arXiv:2507.01467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01467v2)
- iREPA ("What matters for representation alignment", ICLR 2026): across 27 vision encoders, generation quality tracks the *spatial structure* of the target representation (pairwise cosine similarity between patch tokens) rather than its global/linear-probe accuracy. Fix: replace REPA's MLP projector with a convolution and add spatial normalization of target features (<4 lines of code); consistently faster convergence across encoders, sizes, REPA-E, MeanFlow and JiT. [verified; no numbers on abstract page] — [arXiv:2512.10794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10794v1)
- HASTE ("REPA works until it doesn't"): alignment helps early but can hurt late in training; proposes early-stopped/holistic alignment. [title-level, not fetched] — [arXiv:2505.16792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16792v1)
- REPA-G (2026): REPA-trained models can be steered at test time by the gradient of similarity between internal features and reference-image encoder features (global, patch or masked); REPA-E + DINOv2 full-feature guidance FID 1.45 on ImageNet; plain SiT without alignment responds poorly to this guidance. [verified] — [Sereyjol-Garros et al., arXiv:2602.03753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03753v1)
- RAE (representation autoencoders): replaces the VAE with a frozen representation encoder plus trained decoder, reported FID 1.51 without guidance and 1.13 with guidance at ImageNet-256. [background, not re-verified] — [Zheng et al., arXiv:2510.11690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11690)
- No representation-alignment paper for text/discrete diffusion LMs was found; the REPA-G paper surfaced by a text-focused search is image-only. [verified absence in searched results] — [arXiv:2602.03753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03753v1)

### Inferences
- Concrete idea E (REPA for the drafter): during training, the target's hidden states over the drafted block are available for free (one target forward over the gold continuation, which is already needed to produce training features). Add a loss aligning a middle drafter layer's states at drafted positions (through a small projector) to Qwen3-8B hidden states at those positions. This asks the drafter to *predict* the target computation it lacks, rather than receive it. Zero inference cost, and REPA/REPA-E suggest large training-speed gains.
- iREPA transfers as: align relational structure across the 16 positions (token-token cosine similarity matrix, or a 1D conv projector plus per-position normalization) rather than per-token MLP regression. That fits the observation that exact feature values from the estimator are noisy while relative structure may be more reliable.
- Choice of alignment target: Qwen3-8B states at gold positions (strong teacher, but mismatched when the draft diverges from gold), or the 0.6B estimator's states over the drafter's own tokens (weaker, but matches inference). Your "features after a wrong token are useless" finding argues for aligning to target states on gold tokens and masking the loss after the first position where draft and gold diverge, if training on self-drafted sequences.
- REG analog (concrete idea F): append one or a few "summary" tokens to the block that the drafter must denoise jointly with the 16 draft tokens, with targets = pooled Qwen3-8B hidden state over the block (or the next-verified-position state). REG shows one entangled high-level token gives larger gains than an alignment loss in images.
- HASTE suggests annealing the alignment loss off later in training; plan a schedule.
- REPA-G suggests that once aligned, the drafter's internal states become steerable at test time by the estimator's features via a similarity gradient, but a gradient step at inference is likely too costly for drafting; the conditioning route (K/V injection) is cheaper.

### Gaps
- No evidence found that REPA-style alignment transfers to discrete/text diffusion or to speculative drafters; this is untested.
- Numbers for REPA, VA-VAE and RAE were not re-fetched this session.
- HASTE numbers not retrieved.

## Q4. Conditioning on noisy, partial or approximate context; robust and uncertainty-aware conditioning

### Takeaway
The established recipe for unreliable conditioning is to *train on corrupted conditioning and tell the model how corrupted it is*: cascaded diffusion and Imagen add Gaussian noise to the low-res conditioning image and feed the augmentation level as an input, which was critical to cascade quality. 2025 work adds learned pseudo-conditions for extremely noisy labels and corruption-aware training for video. This matches your finding that low-bit noisy features beat missing features, and suggests conditioning the drafter explicitly on a per-position reliability signal.

### Cited Findings
- Cascaded Diffusion Models: conditioning augmentation (Gaussian noise and blur on the low-resolution conditioning input during training) was critical for sample quality, reducing train-test mismatch between ground-truth low-res inputs and model-generated ones; reported ImageNet-256 FID 4.88 without any classifier. [background, not re-verified] — [Ho et al., arXiv:2106.15282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15282)
- Imagen: noise conditioning augmentation on super-resolution stages, with the augmentation level given to the model as a conditioning input and selectable at inference. [background, not re-verified] — [Saharia et al., arXiv:2205.11487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11487)
- Classifier guidance uses classifiers trained on noisy x_t, so the guidance signal is defined for the noise level actually seen at sampling. [background] — [Dhariwal & Nichol, arXiv:2105.05233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)
- CADS: adds scheduled Gaussian noise to the conditioning vector during sampling (annealed to zero) to increase diversity, showing that controlled noise on conditioning can be beneficial at inference. [background, not re-verified] — [Sadat et al., arXiv:2310.17347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17347)
- Robust learning with extremely noisy conditions (2025): learns pseudo-conditions as surrogates for clean conditions, refined during training by temporal ensembling, plus Reverse-time Diffusion Condition (diffusing the pseudo-conditions) to exploit early memorization; reports SOTA across noise levels on class-conditional image generation and visuomotor policies, and notes existing noise-robust methods fail at high noise. No numbers on the abstract page. [verified] — [arXiv:2510.10149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10149v1)
- Corruption-aware training of latent video diffusion for robust text-to-video (2025). [title-level, not fetched] — [arXiv:2505.21545](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21545v1)
- Ctrl-U: uncertainty-aware reward modeling for controllable generation, down-weighting reward feedback where the reward model is uncertain. [title-level, not fetched] — [arXiv:2410.11236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11236)
- A-CFG and PRISM (Q1, Q2) are uncertainty-aware in the sense that per-token confidence decides where to guide or revise. [verified] — [arXiv:2505.20199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20199); [arXiv:2510.01384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01384v3)

### Inferences
- Your low-bit result is a direct instance of conditioning augmentation: the drafter learned to use imperfect features because it saw them in training. Concrete idea G: train the drafter with a mixture of feature sources at drafted positions (true target states, quantized target states, estimator outputs, Gaussian-noised target states at random levels, and dropped), and feed an embedding of the source/noise level per position (Imagen-style aug-level conditioning). At inference, pass the estimator's level. This lets one drafter degrade gracefully between "oracle" and "none".
- Concrete idea H (per-position reliability): the estimator (0.6B + map) can output a per-position uncertainty (e.g., predicted error norm, trained against true target states), injected as a scalar gate on that position's K/V or as an extra embedding. Since features after a wrong drafted token are useless, the reliability should also depend on the drafter's own confidence at earlier positions: gate_i = f(est. uncertainty_i, min drafter confidence over j < i).
- CADS-style idea: small noise on the estimated features at inference could reduce overconfidence of the drafter on bad estimates; cheap to test.
- The pseudo-condition approach (2510.10149) suggests a learned correction of estimator features (the "learned map") could be trained jointly with the drafter rather than separately, letting the drafter define what the map should output (end-to-end, REPA-E-style, with an alignment loss to keep the map grounded in target states).

### Gaps
- No paper found on uncertainty-aware conditioning that propagates a per-token reliability score from an auxiliary model into a diffusion denoiser; this appears open.
- No quantitative numbers retrieved for 2510.10149, 2505.21545 or Ctrl-U.
- No found work on conditioning that becomes invalid after an upstream error (the "features after a wrong token are useless" phenomenon); closest analogs are speculative-decoding training-time-test methods (e.g., EAGLE-3), which were outside this scope and not researched here.
