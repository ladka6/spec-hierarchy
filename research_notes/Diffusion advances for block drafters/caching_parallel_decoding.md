# Caching, Feature Reuse/Forecasting, and Parallel/Speculative Decoding for Diffusion Models (focus: diffusion LMs), with transfer ideas for a block-diffusion drafter

Scope note: papers dated before 2025 are marked [background]. Every arXiv ID below was seen in a search result or fetched page in this session unless explicitly marked "(ID not verified this session)".

## Q1. Feature caching across denoising steps in image/video diffusion (reuse vs forecast)

### Takeaway
The field moved from "cache-then-reuse" (DeepCache, FORA, Delta-DiT, TeaCache, ToCa, FasterCache) to "cache-then-forecast" (TaylorSeer and its successors HiCache, FoCa, AB-Cache, FreqCa), which roughly doubled the usable skip interval: TaylorSeer gets about 5x near-lossless on FLUX and HunyuanVideo with first-order extrapolation from finite differences of a few fully computed steps, and SpeCa adds a cheap forecast-then-verify loop (speculative-decoding style) to push to 6-7x.

### Cited Findings
Taxonomy and reuse-style methods
- A 2025 survey of diffusion caching (arXiv 2510.19755) classifies methods into static caching (fixed schedule) and dynamic caching (timestep-adaptive, layer-adaptive, predictive, hybrid), and describes the predictive class as the move from "Cache-Then-Reuse" to "Cache-Then-Forecast". [Survey 2510.19755](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19755)
- Mechanisms per the survey [background unless noted]: DeepCache reuses U-Net upsampling (high-level) features at a fixed interval; FORA caches attention and MLP outputs of DiT blocks at fixed intervals; Delta-DiT caches rear blocks early in sampling and front blocks late, caching residuals (deltas) rather than outputs; FasterCache caches CFG unconditional outputs with frequency-domain filtering; ToCa does token-level caching where a scoring rule picks which tokens to recompute; TeaCache uses an input-signal (timestep-embedding-modulated input) estimate of output change and refreshes when the accumulated estimate crosses a threshold; MagCache uses a residual-magnitude-decay error model with no offline fitting; EasyCache gates fully online and refreshes when accumulated error exceeds tau. [Survey 2510.19755](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19755)
- The survey itself gives no speedup numbers for DeepCache, FORA, Delta-DiT, TeaCache, ToCa, TaylorSeer, FasterCache, FoCa, MagCache, or EasyCache. [Survey 2510.19755](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19755)

Forecasting methods
- TaylorSeer (arXiv 2503.06923, "From Reusing to Forecasting"): predicts features at skipped steps by a Taylor expansion whose derivatives are forward finite differences over fully computed steps spaced N apart; order m needs m+1 fully computed steps; training-free. [TaylorSeer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06923)
- TaylorSeer results: FLUX.1-dev 50 steps, N=6, O=1: 4.99x (3719.5T to 744.8T FLOPs), ImageReward 0.9953 vs 0.9898 baseline, CLIP 19.637 vs 19.604; N=5, O=2: 4.16x, ImageReward 1.0296. HunyuanVideo N=5, O=1: 5.00x FLOPs reduction, VBench 79.93% vs 80.66%; N=6: 5.56x at 79.78%. DiT-XL/2 ImageNet 256: FID 2.34 at 2.77x (N=3, O=3), FID 2.65 at 4.53x (N=5, O=3), better than ToCa and DuCa at that ratio. [TaylorSeer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06923)
- TaylorSeer order ablation: on DiT, gains saturate after third order; first order is used for the large FLUX/HunyuanVideo models. [TaylorSeer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06923)
- Successors (2025): AB-Cache (Adams-Bashforth linear recursion); HiCache (arXiv 2508.16984; scaled Hermite polynomial basis for more stable extrapolation; up to 5.93x theoretical and 2.43x wall-clock on InfDiT super-resolution); FoCa (arXiv 2508.16211, "Forecast then Calibrate", treats caching as an ODE, BDF2 extrapolation plus Heun-style correction); FreqCa (reuse low-frequency components, forecast high-frequency components; up to 7.14x on Qwen-Image with 99% memory savings); DiCache/FastCache (probe-based interpolation, static-token approximation); hybrids ClusCa, OmniCache, TokenCache, HyCa, BlockDance. [Survey 2510.19755](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19755); [HiCache](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16984); [FoCa](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2508.16211)
- SpeCa (arXiv 2509.11628, "Speculative Feature Caching"): forecasts features for later timesteps from fully computed references, then a parameter-free verifier accepts or rejects each forecast in real time; verification costs about 1.67% to 3.5% of full inference; compute adapts per sample (easy samples skip more). Results: FLUX 6.34x with about 5.5% quality drop; DiT 7.3x preserving fidelity; HunyuanVideo 6.1x at VBench 79.84%. [SpeCa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11628v1)
- The survey summarizes SpeCa's loop as: draft predictor (e.g., TaylorSeer) extrapolates k steps, lightweight verifier computes relative error e_k, accept if e_k below a dynamic threshold, else roll back to last verified state and recompute; theoretical speedup S ~ 1/((1 - alpha) + gamma) with alpha the acceptance rate and gamma the verification cost ratio. [Survey 2510.19755](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19755)

### Inferences
- Forecasting beats reuse when features drift smoothly and monotonically along the trajectory axis. First order is the robust default for big models; higher orders help only when the underlying trajectory is smooth and the anchor spacing is small.
- The SpeCa pattern (forecast cheaply, verify cheaply with a small fraction of the real computation, fall back on failure) is structurally the same as speculative decoding, and is the most directly transferable idea for the "estimation pass" cost.
- All these methods exploit continuity along the denoising-time axis of a fixed set of tokens. In an AR verify setting the analogous axis is the iteration/round axis at a fixed position, not the position axis; position-wise extrapolation has no analogous smoothness evidence (see Q4).

### Gaps
- Primary-paper speedup numbers for DeepCache (arXiv 2312.00858), FORA (2407.01425), Delta-DiT (2406.01125), TeaCache (2411.19108), ToCa (2410.05317), FasterCache (2410.19355) were not fetched this session; these IDs are from background knowledge and not verified this session. Commonly reported figures (DeepCache about 2.3x on SD1.5, TeaCache about 4.4x on Open-Sora-Plan) are recalled, not verified.
- FoCa and HiCache quantitative results beyond the survey summary were not fetched.
- 2026 image/video caching successors were not specifically searched; the survey is the latest consolidated source found.

## Q2. Caching and staleness handling for diffusion LMs

### Takeaway
dLLM caching works because KV/feature states of prompt and already-decoded tokens change slowly across denoising steps (cosine 0.84-1.00 within a block in LLaDA), while masked/undecided tokens and deep layers drift more. Methods differ mainly in the refresh rule: periodic per-block (Fast-dLLM), interval + similarity-selected subset (dLLM-Cache V-verify), one-step-delayed (dKV-Cache, Sparse-dLLM), attention-drift triggered with depth-aware partial recompute (Elastic-Cache), or attention/certainty-selected tokens (d2Cache). Combined with parallel decoding, reported speedups reach 25-48x over vanilla dLLM decoding on long GSM8K generations.

### Cited Findings
- Fast-dLLM (arXiv 2505.22618, ICLR 2026): block-wise approximate KV cache for bidirectional dLLMs; prefix cache stores prompt + earlier blocks, DualCache also caches the masked suffix. Staleness handled by recomputing all KV after each block completes; within a block, cache is reused across denoising steps. Evidence: KV cosine similarity across adjacent steps about 0.84-1.00 for the prompt and 0.88-1.00 for the last block in LLaDA-Instruct, dropping for distant steps. [Fast-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22618v2)
- Fast-dLLM results (A100, bs 1): up to 27.6x throughput on LLaDA GSM8K 8-shot at length 1024 (0.7 to 19.3 tok/s), parallel decoding alone 13.3x there; GSM8K 5-shot length 256: 6.7 to 54.4 tok/s (8.1x), accuracy 79.3 to 78.5; Dream-Base GSM8K: 5.3x, 75.0 to 74.8; LLaDA-V MathVista 9.9x with accuracy 59.2 to 56.6. [Fast-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22618v2)
- dLLM-Cache (arXiv 2506.06295, ICML 2026): long-interval prompt cache (prompt K, V, attention and FFN outputs refreshed every K_p = 25-100 steps), short-interval response cache (full refresh every K_r = 1-8 steps); in between, V-verify recomputes only the rho = 25% of response tokens whose current Value vectors have lowest cosine similarity to cached Values. Observation: Value-vector similarity tracks attention/FFN output change. Results: up to 8.08x on LLaDA-8B GPQA (22.07T to 2.73T FLOPs/token, no loss), 6.90x Dream GSM8K, up to 9.1x; cosine selection 78.54% vs L2 55.95% on GSM8K; value-based selection beats random. [dLLM-Cache](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06295v1)
- dKV-Cache (arXiv 2505.15781): one-step delayed caching (a token's KV is cached at the step after it is decoded) plus a refresh mechanism; in Sparse-dLLM's comparison on LLaDA-8B-Instruct it averages 59.96 accuracy at about 1.4x throughput and about 1.2x memory. [Sparse-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02558v2); [d2Cache](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23094v2)
- Sparse-dLLM (arXiv 2508.02558, AAAI): dLLM attention is sparse and the set of salient (heavily attended) tokens is stable across steps; evicts low-attention prefix and suffix KV entries (scored by current-block queries, max-pooled), delays caching by one step because out-of-block KV changes noticeably between the first two steps, full refresh at block change. Up to 10x throughput claimed; LLaDA-8B average 59.86 accuracy vs 59.24 baseline at about 3.4x throughput, similar memory. [Sparse-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02558v2)
- d2Cache (arXiv 2509.23094, ICLR 2026): caches KV of most tokens, recomputes a small adaptive subset each step: stage 1 picks top-k=32 masked tokens by certainty prior (Gaussian-weighted density of nearby known tokens) times confidence; stage 2 picks the smallest high-influence set by attention rollout with cumulative mass p=0.1. The certainty prior also gives a quasi left-to-right decoding order. Average 3.5x over vanilla on LLaDA-Instruct and Dream-Instruct with accuracy at or above vanilla (vs dLLM-Cache 2.3x/2.1x and Fast-dLLM 2.7x/2.6x in the same table); up to 48.3x with parallel decoding on LLaDA MBPP. [d2Cache](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23094v2)
- Elastic-Cache (arXiv 2510.14973, "Attention Is All You Need for KV Cache in Diffusion LLMs"): per layer, tracks the most-attended decoded token's attention weights between steps; if cosine similarity drops below gamma = 0.9, recompute KV from the next layer to the last layer, keep shallow-layer caches (KV drift grows with depth). Only a sliding window of leftmost masked positions is predicted; distant MASK KVs are reused. Results: LLaDA-Instruct GSM8K 512: about 25.2x (90.1 tok/s) at 77.71% vs 77.10%; LLaDA-1.5 GSM8K 512: up to 45.1x at 81.35% (matching baseline), 8.7x at 256 tokens; Dream-7B GSM8K 512: 21.4x at 75.6% vs 76.0%; HumanEval about 5x. [Elastic-Cache](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14973)

### Inferences
- Common finding across dLLM papers: (a) stable tokens are those with settled context (prompt, decoded tokens); (b) drift concentrates in deep layers and near the frontier of newly decoded tokens; (c) a cheap staleness probe (Value cosine, attention-weight cosine on one representative token, attention rollout) is enough to decide refresh. These map onto the drafter setting: positions before the first changed draft token are "settled", positions after a changed token are "stale".
- Elastic-Cache's depth-aware partial recompute (shallow layers reused, recompute from layer l onward) suggests the 0.6B estimator could reuse its own early-layer states across draft iterations and recompute only deeper layers for changed positions.
- Speedup magnitudes for dLLMs are relative to very slow vanilla dLLM decoding (single-digit tok/s), so they do not translate into comparable gains for an AR target with a normal KV cache.

### Gaps
- Fast-dLLM v2 (arXiv 2509.26328; ID not verified this session) was not fetched; I recall it adapts a pretrained AR model (Qwen2.5) to block diffusion with a hierarchical block/sub-block cache and reports about 2.5x over AR decoding, but this is unverified.
- No dLLM caching paper found that forecasts (extrapolates) features in TaylorSeer style; all dLLM methods reuse plus selectively refresh. I did not find a source confirming or ruling out a TaylorSeer-style dLLM method.

## Q3. Parallel decoding and speculative decoding for/with diffusion LMs

### Takeaway
Parallel unmasking is governed by confidence (Fast-dLLM threshold, with a theorem that high marginal confidence makes product-of-marginals greedy equal joint greedy), learned filters (Learn2PD), or a small AR model's joint density (APD). On the speculative side, diffusion models are now strong drafters for AR targets: DFlash (target-feature-conditioned block diffusion drafter, 5 layers, 16-token blocks) reaches tau about 6.5 and 4.9x on Qwen3-8B; DiffuSpec uses an off-the-shelf DLM training-free (up to 3x); Uno/UnoQwen (Sep 2026) puts LoRA diffusion weights inside the target and reports higher acceptance than both DFlash and EAGLE-3 at sampling temperature 1. Self-speculation inside block-diffusion LMs (S2D2) uses block-size-1 mode as verifier.

### Cited Findings
Parallel decoding within dLLMs
- Fast-dLLM confidence-aware parallel decoding: unmask all positions with max-prob above tau = 0.9 (at least one per step). Theorem 1: if each marginal confidence exceeds 1 - eps and (n+1) eps <= 1, greedy decoding from the product of marginals equals greedy from the true joint; TV bound below (3n-1) eps / 2. [Fast-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22618v2)
- APD (arXiv 2506.00413, NeurIPS 2025): runs Dream-7B left to right, samples several tokens in parallel, and uses a small AR model (Qwen2.5-0.5B) to decide how many to keep: target p_T proportional to p_D^R * p_AR^(1-R), shared-Gumbel (universal coupling) sampling, accept until the first disagreement (first token always accepted). The paper frames it as inverted speculative decoding (small model checks big model's draft). Knobs: R (mixture weight), W (KV recompute window), M (max mask lookahead). Results: more than 5 tokens per iteration on average at about 80% GSM8K accuracy; mean 7.62 tokens/step (R=0.7, W=16, M=100) and 5.75 (R=0.6, W=32, M=200); over 100 tok/s possible at some quality cost; Dream-7B L2R baseline 0.832 at 10.1 tok/s vs Qwen2.5-7B AR 0.854 at 38.6 tok/s. APD is lossy and does not raise the dLLM's quality ceiling. [APD](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00413v2)
- Learn2PD (arXiv 2509.25188, ICLR 2026): trains a lightweight filter model post hoc (minute-level GPU time) that predicts per position whether the current prediction already equals the final output, plus End-of-Text Prediction to stop decoding padding. Up to 22.58x on LLaDA without performance drop, 57.51x combined with KV cache. Filter size/inputs not given in abstract. [Learn2PD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25188v2)
- d2Cache certainty-prior decoding order (decode masked tokens near known tokens first) improves NAR LLaDA average from 36.0 (confidence-based) to 50.3. [d2Cache](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23094v2)

Self-speculation in diffusion LMs
- S2D2 (arXiv 2603.25702): training-free self-speculation for block-diffusion LMs; block-diffusion mode drafts, block-size-1 (AR) mode of the same model verifies with min(1, q/p) acceptance and residual resampling; a special attention mask scores all drafted tokens in one pass for position-aligned models; routing policies (min-span, score threshold, hysteresis, contextual bandit) decide when verification is worth it. Up to 4.7x over AR decoding on SDAR and 1.57x over a tuned dynamic-decoding baseline with up to 4.5 points higher accuracy; LLaDA2.1-Mini 4.4x over static baseline. Not claimed to be globally lossless. [S2D2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25702v1)
- An ICLR 2026 paper titled "Self-Speculative Decoding Accelerates Lossless Inference in Any-Order and Any-Subset Autoregressive Models" exists (search hit; likely arXiv 2510.04147, not fetched). [ICLR 2026 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/d29b8d53678015079e1d245c023e49d2-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2510.04147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04147v1)

Diffusion models as drafters for AR targets
- SpecDiff / Speculative Diffusion Decoding (Christopher et al., arXiv 2408.05636) [background]: discrete diffusion model drafts for AR verification, with a trained/calibrated drafter. [SpecDiff](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.05636v1); characterization per [DiffuSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02358v1)
- DiffuSpec (arXiv 2510.02358, ACL 2026 Findings): training-free, a pretrained DLM (Dream-7B appears in figures) drafts a block in one forward pass; causal-consistency path search builds a top-M token lattice pruned by cumulative mass tau (e.g., 0.8), and beam-searches left to right scoring paths with DLM confidence plus a small causal proxy (e.g., n-gram); an adaptive draft-length controller uses recent acceptance. Up to 3x wall-clock. [DiffuSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02358v1)
- DFlash (arXiv 2602.06036): 5-layer drafter (8 for Qwen3-Coder), block 16, single forward pass per block; shares frozen target embedding and LM head; hidden states from 5 target layers (uniformly from layer 2 to third-to-last) are concatenated, projected, and injected into K and V of every drafter layer, stored in the drafter KV cache and reused across drafting iterations. Without target conditioning a 5-layer diffusion drafter got only about 2-3x. Training: about 800K samples with target-generated responses, random anchors starting masked blocks, block-sparse attention via FlexAttention, position-decayed loss w_k = exp(-(k-1)/gamma). Qwen3-8B (thinking off): temp 0: 4.86x, tau 6.49 vs EAGLE-3 tree-16 1.76x/2.96; temp 1: 4.03x, tau 5.48; up to 6.08x on MATH-500; up to 5.1x on SGLang B200, decreasing with concurrency. [DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v1)
- Uno / UnoQwen (arXiv 2609.04010, Sep 2026, Sahoo et al.): no separate drafter; rank-128 gated LoRA "diffusion weights" in every layer of the frozen AR model draft a block in one pass; trained by diffusion distillation with discrete consistency loss and a total-variation loss that targets acceptance length; the unmodified AR path verifies with speculative rejection sampling (lossless); drafter and verifier share one KV cache. UnoQwen (Qwen3-8B) at temp 1: tau 3.89 (linear, B=4) vs EAGLE-3 2.08 and DFlash 2.07; per-request-optimal tau 5.97 vs 3.48 and 2.74; per-request throughput 445 vs 284 and 370 tok/s; extra params 0.35B vs DFlash 1.05B; about 2.5x per-request speedup. [Uno](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04010)
- DARTree: "Speculative Diffusion Decoding with Autoregressive Draft Trees" (arXiv 2608.13524) appeared in search; not fetched. [DARTree](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2608.13524)

### Inferences
- Note the measurement conflict: DFlash reports tau 5.48 at temp 1 on Qwen3-8B, while Uno's evaluation reports DFlash tau 2.74 (per-request-optimal) at temp 1 on a different test set (OpenThoughts-style 1K/8K, likely thinking/reasoning outputs). Differences in data, thinking mode, and evaluation setup likely explain the gap; any comparison should be run on the same setup.
- Uno is the closest published attack on the same "drafter is context-limited" problem: by living inside the target, its draft pathway has the target's full KV cache and per-layer computation, which is exactly what the 1B external drafter lacks. It is a competitor baseline for the estimate-and-redraft idea, and a strong argument that the gap is real.
- APD and DiffuSpec both use a cheap causal model as an acceptance proxy for parallel drafts; this is a validated pattern for choosing draft length or truncation point before paying for a verify.

### Gaps
- DiffuSpec target models and acceptance lengths were not in the fetched excerpt.
- No paper found that iterates draft -> estimate target states -> redraft before verification; SpeCa (in image space) and S2D2 (self-verification routing) are the closest analogues.
- DARTree and the any-order self-speculation paper were not read.

## Q4. Transfer ideas for the DFlash-style drafter with estimated target states

### Takeaway
The most promising transfers are (1) SpeCa-style forecast-then-verify on the estimation pass, gated by a cheap staleness probe; (2) dLLM-cache-style selective refresh: recompute estimated states only from the first position where the draft changed (prefix states are reusable exactly), and only in deeper layers; (3) round-axis (not position-axis) TaylorSeer extrapolation of the estimator's output; (4) APD/DiffuSpec-style small-AR density as a truncation/acceptance proxy, which the 0.6B estimator already computes for free.

### Cited Findings (evidence each idea rests on)
- Causal prefix reuse is exact in AR targets; dLLM work had to approximate it because attention is bidirectional (Fast-dLLM refreshes per block; dKV-Cache delays by one step). [Fast-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22618v2); [Sparse-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02558v2)
- Selective refresh by cheap similarity (Value cosine, 25% of tokens) loses nothing on most dLLM tasks; cosine beats L2 as selector. [dLLM-Cache](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06295v1)
- Drift grows with depth; recomputing only from the first drifting layer onward preserves accuracy. [Elastic-Cache](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14973)
- Forecast-then-verify with verifier cost 1.7-3.5% of full compute gives 6-7x in DiTs; S ~ 1/((1 - alpha) + gamma). [SpeCa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11628v1); [Survey 2510.19755](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19755)
- First-order Taylor on finite differences of 2 anchors is near-lossless at 5x for large DiTs; higher order helps only with smooth trajectories. [TaylorSeer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06923)
- Small AR density times big model marginals (p_D^R p_AR^(1-R)) as accept-until-disagreement rule yields 5-7 tokens/step. [APD](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00413v2)
- DFlash already stores injected target features in the drafter KV cache and reuses them across drafting iterations, so new estimated-state K/V for drafted positions can be appended without recomputing the verified-context K/V. [DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v1)

### Inferences (concrete proposals, not from sources)
1. Prefix-exact incremental estimation. In draft round r+1, find the first index j where the new draft differs from round r. Estimated states for positions < j are identical (causal estimator), so recompute only positions >= j. Combined with the user's finding that states after a wrong token are useless, this makes the estimation cost proportional to the changed suffix, and suggests stopping the iteration when j stops moving (fixed point) or when j exceeds the expected acceptance length.
2. Round-axis forecasting instead of running the 0.6B model. For positions whose token is unchanged but whose left context changed only far away, extrapolate h_t^(r+1) from h_t^(r), h_t^(r-1) with first-order finite differences (TaylorSeer O=1). Expect this to work only for positions after a "settled" prefix; because states after a changed token change discontinuously (token identity changes), forecasting should be limited to unchanged-token positions, and position-axis extrapolation (h_{t+1} from h_t, h_{t-1}) has no smoothness evidence and is unlikely to work for discrete token streams.
3. SpeCa-style verification of the estimate. Run the 0.6B estimator only on a few layers (or only the map's input layer) as a probe, compare to the cached/forecast state with cosine similarity (dLLM-Cache's V-verify analogue); recompute full estimate only where similarity drops below threshold. Budget model: if probe cost gamma is about 5-10% of an estimation pass and acceptance alpha of reuse is high, estimation cost falls toward gamma.
4. Depth-aware reuse inside the estimator (Elastic-Cache). Keep shallow-layer KV of the 0.6B model for unchanged tokens across iterations and recompute only deep layers, since the learned map likely reads deep features.
5. Exploit the 4-bit finding as a "draft-target" in a 3-stage cascade: since a 4-bit target reproduces hidden states almost exactly, it can serve as both the estimator and a pre-verifier (S2D2-style routing: verify with the full target only when the pre-verifier's predicted acceptance is high enough to justify cost). This connects directly to the hierarchical speculative decoding framing.
6. Small-AR density as truncation proxy. The 0.6B estimator already produces next-token logits over drafted tokens; use APD's p_D^R p_AR^(1-R) accept-until-disagreement rule (or DiffuSpec's lattice path search with the 0.6B as causal scorer) to truncate the draft before target verify and to decide whether another redraft iteration is worth it. Compute the agreement index j* between drafter top-1 and 0.6B top-1 as a free stopping/staleness signal.
7. Learned refresh policy. Learn2PD shows a tiny filter trained in minutes can predict which positions are already final; an analogous filter could predict, per drafted position, whether its estimated state is stale (needs refresh) or whether a redraft will change it, gating the iteration.
8. Competitive baseline to report: Uno/UnoQwen (target-internal LoRA drafter sharing the target KV) addresses the same missing-context gap; the iterative-estimation approach should be compared against it on the same Qwen3-8B setup and temperature.

### Gaps
- No source measures how fast LLM hidden states change across speculative rounds at a fixed position, so the smoothness assumption for round-axis forecasting is untested; the user's own 4-bit result is the only evidence and should be complemented by measuring cosine(h^(r), h^(r+1)) for unchanged-token positions.
- No source evaluates feature forecasting for discrete-token AR models; all forecasting evidence is from continuous diffusion trajectories in DiTs.
