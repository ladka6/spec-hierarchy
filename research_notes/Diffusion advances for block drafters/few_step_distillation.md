# Few-step and one-step sampling/distillation for diffusion and flow models, and transfer to a block-diffusion speculative drafter

Scope note: 2025-2026 work is the focus. Items marked [BACKGROUND] are 2022-2024 foundations. Numbers are those reported by the papers' abstracts/pages as fetched in this session; anything not verified is put under Gaps.

Drafter context assumed for transfer ideas: DFlash-style ~1B, 5-layer drafter that drafts 16 tokens in one forward pass, conditioned on Qwen3-8B hidden features of verified positions (injected as K/V in every layer). Refinement loop today: draft -> estimate target hidden states over drafted tokens with Qwen3-0.6B + linear map -> redraft, then one target verification. Each refinement step costs one context-estimation pass.

## Q1. Continuous-domain few-step methods (consistency/sCM/ECT, shortcut, MeanFlow/flow maps, IMM, DMD/DMD2, ADD, rectified flow): what each does and headline result

### Takeaway
The 2025-2026 frontier has split into (a) trained-from-scratch "two-time" models that learn the jump between any two noise levels (MeanFlow, IMM, shortcut, flow maps), reaching ImageNet-256 FID 3.43 at 1 NFE and 1.99 at 8 steps, and (b) distillation of large teachers, where pure distribution matching (DMD2) gives top one-step quality but collapses diversity, and 2025's rCM fixes this by combining continuous-time consistency with a DMD-style score regularizer, scaling to 14B video models at 1-4 steps. The most transferable ideas for a drafter are: condition on the step size/number of refinement rounds, train on the student's own rollouts (backward simulation / self forcing), and mix a consistency/self-consistency target with a mode-seeking distribution-matching term.

### Cited Findings
Consistency family
- [BACKGROUND] Consistency models (Song et al., 2023, arXiv:2303.01469) learn a map from any point on the PF-ODE trajectory to its origin, enabling 1-2 step sampling; ECT "Consistency Models Made Easy" (arXiv:2406.14548) is the 2024 cheap fine-tune-from-diffusion variant. Mechanism only, numbers not re-verified here. — [CM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469), [ECT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14548)
- sCM (Lu & Song, OpenAI; ICLR 2025 oral, arXiv:2410.11081) simplifies, stabilizes and scales continuous-time consistency models. 2-step FID: CIFAR-10 2.06, ImageNet-64 1.48, ImageNet-512 1.88 with a 1.5B model (sCD-XXL), vs. teacher-level diffusion FIDs of 2.01 / 1.33 / 1.73, i.e. within about 10% of the best diffusion models with 2 steps. — [arXiv:2410.11081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11081), [ICLR 2025 oral](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2025/oral/31868), [OpenAI blog](https://openai.com/index/simplifying-stabilizing-and-scaling-continuous-time-consistency-models/)
- rCM (NVIDIA + Tsinghua, ICLR 2026, arXiv:2510.08431): score-regularized continuous-time consistency. Adds score distillation (DMD-style reverse-divergence) as a "long-skip regularizer" to sCM, fixing sCM's quality problems while keeping diversity. Applied to Cosmos-Predict2 T2I (0.6B/2B/14B) and Wan2.1 T2V (1.3B/14B); default 4 steps, 1 step works for T2I and 2 steps for T2V; 15x-50x sampling acceleration; "matches or even surpasses DMD2" on GenEval/VBench with noticeably more diverse outputs (DMD2 collapses layouts/motion). — [arXiv:2510.08431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08431), [project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/rcm/)

Two-time / flow-map models (trained from scratch)
- [BACKGROUND, late 2024] Shortcut models (Frans et al., arXiv:2410.12557) condition the network on both the noise level and the desired step size d, and train with a self-consistency target (one step of size 2d should equal two steps of size d), allowing 1-step or multi-step sampling from one network with a single training run. Headline numbers not re-verified in this session (fetch blocked). — [arXiv:2410.12557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12557)
- MeanFlow (Geng et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2505.13447) models the average velocity over an interval [r, t] instead of the instantaneous velocity. A derived "MeanFlow identity" gives a regression target that needs only the instantaneous velocity and a Jacobian-vector product (with stop-gradient). No pretraining, distillation or curriculum. 1-NFE ImageNet-256 FID 3.43 (XL/2), with B/2, M/2, L/2 at 6.17, 5.01, 3.84; CFG is folded into the target so sampling stays 1 NFE. — [arXiv:2505.13447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13447), [NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/6d13e085b79d454da5910e4ca82a3d9d-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Inductive Moment Matching (IMM, Zhou, Ermon, Song; arXiv:2503.07565): single-stage training of a one/few-step model via moment matching between distributions at different times, trained from scratch. ImageNet-256 FID 1.99 with 8 steps (beats diffusion models, standard transformer); CIFAR-10 FID 1.98 with 2 steps (SOTA from scratch). — [arXiv:2503.07565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07565)

Distribution matching / adversarial
- [BACKGROUND] DMD (Yin et al., 2023, arXiv:2311.18828): one-step generator trained so its output distribution matches the teacher's, using the difference between a "real" (teacher) score and a "fake" score of the student's own distribution (approx. reverse-KL gradient), plus a regression loss on teacher noise-image pairs. — [arXiv:2311.18828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18828)
- [BACKGROUND, 2024] DMD2 (arXiv:2405.14867) removes the regression loss, uses a two time-scale update rule (train the fake critic more often) for stability, adds a GAN loss on real data, and supports multi-step students trained with backward simulation (training inputs are generated by running the student itself, removing train/inference mismatch). ImageNet-64 one-step FID 1.28; zero-shot COCO FID 8.35 (student beats teacher at about 500x less inference cost); 4-step SDXL distillation surpassing the teacher. — [arXiv:2405.14867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14867)
- [BACKGROUND] Adversarial Diffusion Distillation (ADD, arXiv:2311.17042, SDXL-Turbo) and Latent ADD (arXiv:2403.12015, SD3-Turbo) combine score distillation with an adversarial loss to reach 1-4 steps. Numbers not re-verified here. — [ADD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17042), [LADD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12015)
- [BACKGROUND] Rectified flow / reflow (Liu et al., arXiv:2209.03003; InstaFlow arXiv:2309.06380) retrains on (noise, teacher-sample) couplings to straighten trajectories so few Euler steps suffice. Numbers not re-verified here. — [Rectified flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003), [InstaFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06380)

Video / sequential
- Self Forcing (arXiv:2506.08009): trains autoregressive video diffusion by rolling out autoregressively with KV cache during training, conditioning each frame on the model's own previous outputs (attacks exposure bias), supervised with a holistic video-level loss, using a few-step diffusion model plus stochastic gradient truncation to keep cost manageable. Reports real-time streaming generation with sub-second latency on one GPU, matching or exceeding slower non-causal models. — [arXiv:2506.08009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08009)

### Inferences
- The "two-time" parameterization (shortcut d, MeanFlow (r,t), IMM) maps naturally to a drafter conditioned on "how many refresh rounds to jump": a single network that can be queried for k=0 (raw draft) or k=K (post-refinement draft), with self-consistency between "two jumps of k" and "one jump of 2k" as the training signal. This needs no teacher beyond the drafter's own refinement loop.
- DMD2's backward simulation and Self Forcing are the continuous analogue of the drafter's real problem: the drafter is trained on teacher-forced context but at inference conditions on its own (estimated) context. Training on rolled-out, self-generated intermediate states is the consistent fix across modalities.
- rCM's lesson (consistency alone loses quality, distribution matching alone loses diversity, combine them) suggests a combined loss for a distilled drafter: regression to the k-round refined draft (consistency target) plus a reverse-KL/acceptance-aware term toward the true target LLM distribution (distribution-matching target). For speculative decoding, mode collapse matters less than for image generation under greedy verification, so the mode-seeking term is probably safe and useful.

### Gaps
- Audio-specific few-step methods (e.g., consistency/flow-map distillation for TTS or music) were not searched within the tool budget; no audio results are reported here.
- Exact headline numbers for shortcut models, ADD/LADD, rectified flow/InstaFlow and ECT were not re-verified this session (fetch restrictions/budget). Only mechanism and arXiv IDs are given.
- Whether Self Forcing's "holistic loss" is specifically DMD/SiD/GAN was not confirmed from the fetched abstract.
- 2026 MeanFlow successors (e.g., improved/"alpha"-flow or flow-map distillation papers) were not covered; a 2026 popular-press item mentions "Flow Maps 2026" but was not treated as a source.

## Q2. Which few-step methods have been applied to discrete / text diffusion, and results

### Takeaway
Discrete distillation is now active: one-step distillation of masked image models (Di[M]O, FPD) reaches near-teacher quality, text-side methods give 8-64x step reductions (SDTT, Duo-DCD) or one step via continuous flow maps (FMLM, DLM-One), and dLLM-specific trajectory distillation (d3LLM, dParallel, T3D) raises tokens per forward to about 5-9 on GSM8K with little accuracy loss. The recurring recipe is: generate teacher trajectories (or the student's own rollouts), then train with a mode-seeking (reverse KL / DDO / distribution matching) objective on on-policy intermediate states.

### Cited Findings
Masked/discrete image models
- Di[M]O (ICCV 2025, arXiv:2503.15457): first one-step distillation of masked diffusion models. Token-level distribution matching: for intermediate states built from the student's own one-step outputs, each masked token's distribution must match the teacher's; an auxiliary model approximates the student distribution to provide gradients through the non-differentiable sampling. Uses a generalized Jeffrey divergence (forward/reverse KL mix, best at coefficient -0.2). Initialization mixes random tokens with 60% masks plus embedding noise to fix the low entropy of an all-[M] start. ImageNet-256 (MaskGit teacher): one-step FID 6.91 / IS 214.0 vs. 16-step teacher 6.60 and 4-step teacher 10.73. Meissonic T2I: one-step HPSv2 28.11 vs. teacher 27.90 at 16 steps; GenEval 0.43 vs. teacher 0.37 at 16 steps and 0.54 at 48 steps. — [arXiv:2503.15457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15457), [ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Zhu_DiMO_Distilling_Masked_Diffusion_Models_into_One-step_Generator_ICCV_2025_paper.html)
- Fixed-Point Distillation (FPD, Wang & Tong, arXiv:2605.21484): treats one-step generation as a fixed-point problem. Student outputs a full draft from a nearly fully masked input; the draft is partially re-masked and refined by one frozen teacher step; the student is trained to match that refinement. Uses a straight-through estimator (hard tokens forward, soft-prob gradients) and a feature-space "drift" loss with in-batch negatives, no auxiliary score network. One-step GenEval 0.45 vs. Di[M]O 0.42 and 16-step teacher 0.48; ImageNet-256 FID 6.90. Ablation: using random drafts instead of the student's own drafts as the refinement source drops GenEval to 0.22. — [arXiv:2605.21484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21484)
- [BACKGROUND] Di4C (arXiv:2410.08709) distills dimensional correlations to cut discrete diffusion steps; cited by Di[M]O as a 4-step baseline (ImageNet FID 6.79, IS 209.2). — [Di4C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08709), [numbers via Di[M]O](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15457)

Text diffusion (small-scale LMs)
- [BACKGROUND, late 2024] SDTT, Self-Distillation Through Time (arXiv:2410.21035): student is trained to match, with reverse KL on per-token log-probs, what the teacher produces in two sampling steps; repeated for 7 rounds of halving steps (10k iterations each). Reports 32-64x fewer steps in the abstract (16-32x in Sec 4.1 for iterated SDTT), generates at least 32 tokens at once while matching/beating GPT-2 nucleus sampling on perplexity, keeps LAMBADA performance; up to 8x faster than an AR model with KV cache at ~1.3B when sampling with 16 steps. Models up to 860M. — [arXiv:2410.21035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21035)
- Duo, "The Diffusion Duality" (arXiv:2506.10892): uniform-state discrete diffusion arises from Gaussian diffusion via argmax, so Gaussian-diffusion tricks transfer. Discrete Consistency Distillation builds deterministic trajectories in Gaussian space and projects with argmax; with a Greedy-Tail sampler, steps go from 1024 to about 8 with little quality loss, and the distilled Duo beats a distilled MDLM in the few-step regime. — [arXiv:2506.10892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10892)
- Flow Map Language Models (FMLM, arXiv:2602.16813): continuous flow over one-hot token embeddings with tokenwise-softmax denoiser and cross-entropy; distilled into a flow map that jumps between distant times. Claims FLM matches SOTA discrete diffusion LMs on LM1B/OWT and one-step FMLM exceeds the 8-step quality of recent few-step discrete diffusion LMs. — [arXiv:2602.16813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16813)
- DLM-One (arXiv:2506.00290): one-step continuous diffusion LM via Score identity Distillation plus an adversarial term (the score estimator doubles as a discriminator, preventing repeated/padding outputs), teacher DiffuSeq. One-step BLEU close to 2000-step teacher (QQP 0.1788 vs 0.1829; Wiki-Auto 0.2927 vs 0.2929), about 500x faster (0.03 s vs 14.94 s per sample), but diversity drops (QG Self-BLEU 0.2789 -> 0.6166). — [arXiv:2506.00290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00290)
- Consistent Diffusion Language Models (CDLM, ICML 2026, OpenReview 64HmKFCaq6): replaces the missing discrete PF-ODE with the exact posterior bridge between noise levels; Multi-Path Discrete Consistency trains a denoiser to be path-invariant in expectation. Single-stage, no teacher; claims SOTA on conditional and unconditional generation, largest gains in few-step regime, up to 32x faster. arXiv ID not found on the page. — [ICML 2026 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/66178)

Large dLLMs (LLaDA/Dream/SDAR-class)
- d3LLM (arXiv:2601.07568): pseudo-trajectory distillation. Teacher dLLM decodes one token per step and only its unmasking order is kept; ground-truth responses are masked in that order and the student is trained with CE, plus curricula over mask ratio (0 -> 0.8) and window (16 -> 32), LoRA rank 256. GSM8K-CoT: d3LLM-LLaDA 9.11 tokens per forward (TPF) at 73.1% (LLaDA 72.6% at TPF 1; dParallel-LLaDA 5.14 TPF at 72.6%); d3LLM-Dream 4.94 TPF at 81.4% (Dream 83.9%). 288.9 tok/s on H100, 5.0x Qwen2.5-7B AR and 10.3x vanilla LLaDA. Pseudo-trajectory masking alone gives ~18% TPF over random masking. — [arXiv:2601.07568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07568)
- dParallel (Chen et al., 2025) uses certainty-forcing distillation; D2F uses discrete diffusion forcing with asymmetric distillation (both described as baselines in d3LLM). — [via d3LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07568)
- T3D (arXiv:2602.12262): trajectory self-distillation for few-step dLLMs. Teacher rollouts (confidence-based unmasking) produce (clean, intermediate) pairs, no ground truth; student initialized from teacher, trained with Direct Discriminative Optimization (DDO, reverse-KL-like, GAN-inspired, likelihood ratio vs. a frozen reference, no discriminator) plus a path-consistency regularizer weighting earlier-decoded tokens more (limits error propagation in block-wise decoding). Argued to be a discrete rectified flow (lower conditional total correlation, lower factorization error). States full-step decoding remains superior but the gap is substantially narrowed. — [arXiv:2602.12262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12262)

### Inferences
- The factorization-error argument in T3D (trajectory distillation lowers conditional total correlation between tokens decoded in parallel) is the exact failure mode of a 16-token parallel drafter: later tokens in the block are drafted independently of the drafter's own earlier tokens. Training the drafter on self-generated, refined targets should reduce this, analogous to reflow.
- FPD is the closest structural match to the drafter's loop: "student drafts in one pass -> partially re-mask/refresh -> one refinement step -> student regresses onto the refined result." Its ablation (student-generated drafts 0.43-0.45 vs random drafts 0.22) argues that the refresh must start from the drafter's own drafts, i.e., on-policy.
- Di[M]O's entropy fix (start from a mix of random tokens and masks plus embedding noise) is relevant if the drafter is run as a one-step generator from an all-mask block; it may help the drafter avoid collapsing to the same block-level mode.
- SDTT's progressive halving (match teacher's 2 steps, then halve again) is directly applicable to the refinement loop: round 1 trains drafter to emulate 2 refresh rounds in 1, round 2 to emulate 4, and so on.

### Gaps
- No arXiv ID was found for CDLM (only OpenReview 64HmKFCaq6) or for dParallel/D2F in this session.
- T3D's numeric results (accuracy at fixed step budgets, models used) were not visible in the fetched text.
- No paper found that distills few-step diffusion specifically into a speculative-decoding drafter (as opposed to a standalone dLLM).

## Q3. How to distill a multi-round "draft -> context refresh -> redraft" process into a single drafter pass

### Takeaway
There is no paper doing exactly this for speculative drafters, but the pieces exist: SDTT/progressive distillation (emulate k steps in 1), FPD (one-step student matched to a teacher refinement of its own draft), shortcut/MeanFlow (condition on jump size), and Draft-OPD (on-policy training of a DFlash drafter with acceptance-aware KL to the target, +13% speedup over DFlash). A practical design is to treat the 0.6B-estimator-in-the-loop system as a teacher, generate its k-round refined blocks, and train the drafter (conditioned only on verified-prefix features) to output them directly, with a target-LLM distribution-matching term on top.

### Cited Findings
- Draft-OPD (arXiv:2605.29343) trains DFlash-style block drafters on-policy: drafter proposes blocks, target verifies, block starts are anchors, drafting is replayed from anchors, and the loss uses forward KL (target -> draft) on accepted tokens and reverse KL on rejected tokens, weighted by gamma^(k-1) with gamma = 0.8 over block position. Initialized from a DFlash SFT checkpoint. On Qwen3 thinking mode at temperature 0, acceptance length rises from 5.35 (DFlash) to 5.85 and average speedup is 4.88x, 13% above DFlash and 23% above EAGLE-3; SGLang serving acceptance length +11.2%, up to 17% throughput on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking. Motivation: SFT on target-generated prefixes plateaus because inference evaluates blocks the drafter itself proposed. — [arXiv:2605.29343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29343)
- SDTT trains the student to match the teacher's 2-step output with reverse KL and iterates halving rounds (7 rounds). — [arXiv:2410.21035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21035)
- FPD's target is "student draft -> partial re-mask -> one teacher refinement step"; matching this drives the student toward a fixed point of the refinement operator. — [arXiv:2605.21484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21484)
- [BACKGROUND] DiMS (arXiv:2206.02999) distills multiple steps of an iterative non-autoregressive MT transformer into fewer steps, an early text precedent for "distill k refinement iterations into one". — [arXiv:2206.02999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02999)
- DMD2 backward simulation: train each generator step on inputs produced by running the student's own earlier steps. — [arXiv:2405.14867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14867)
- Shortcut models condition on step size d and enforce that one 2d step equals two d steps. — [arXiv:2410.12557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12557)

### Inferences (proposed designs; not from any single paper)
1. Unrolled-refinement distillation (SDTT/FPD style). Teacher = current system: drafter + Qwen3-0.6B estimator, run for k rounds. Record final block logits p_k. Train a student drafter (same 1B/5-layer architecture, init from the drafter) with only verified-prefix target features to minimize KL(p_k || student) or reverse KL. Iterate progressively: k=1 -> 2 -> 4 rounds collapsed into one, re-generating teacher targets each round from the latest student (as SDTT does). Cost: zero extra inference-time estimator passes.
2. Fixed-point / self-consistency variant (FPD + shortcut). Train drafter so that D(context) = Refine(D(context)), where Refine is one estimate+redraft round, applied to the drafter's own draft (on-policy, per FPD ablation). Optionally condition the drafter on a round-index embedding k (like shortcut step size d) so one network serves both "cheap raw draft" and "k-equivalent draft", and enforce D(k=2) ~ Refine_once(D(k=1)).
3. Distill the estimator rather than the loop. Since the bottleneck is missing target computation over drafted tokens, add an internal "latent self-conditioning" pass: the drafter's last-layer states over its own draft are fed back as extra K/V (a second, cheap pass inside the 1B drafter), with the 0.6B+linear-map estimates (or true Qwen3-8B states at training time) as regression targets for those K/V. This approximates refinement without an external model; distillation targets come from the true target states.
4. Combine with a target-distribution term (rCM lesson). Loss = consistency/regression to the k-round refined draft + Draft-OPD-style acceptance-aware KL to Qwen3-8B on the drafter's own blocks. The first term transfers the refinement benefit; the second keeps the student anchored to what the verifier actually accepts, preventing the student from inheriting estimator errors.
5. Mode-seeking objectives. T3D's DDO, SDTT reverse KL, and DMD all point to mode-seeking losses for few-step students. Under greedy verification, acceptance depends on matching the target's argmax, so reverse-KL/mode-seeking objectives align with the metric; Draft-OPD's use of reverse KL on rejected tokens is consistent with this.
6. Position weighting. Both T3D (earlier-decoded tokens weighted more) and Draft-OPD (gamma^(k-1)) down-weight later block positions, since acceptance is prefix-based. Use the same weighting in the distilled loss.

### Gaps
- No published work found that distills an external-context-refresh loop (draft -> estimate target states -> redraft) into a one-pass drafter; expected gains are unverified.
- Unknown whether a 5-layer drafter has enough capacity to internalize what 0.6B extra computation provides; the closest evidence (Di[M]O, FPD near-teacher one-step results) is in image generation.

## Q4. Distillation where the teacher has privileged information the student lacks

### Takeaway
Yes: classical Learning Using Privileged Information, and a 2026 LLM-specific paper (Privileged Information Distillation, pi-Distill/OPSD) show that a teacher conditioned on privileged information can be distilled into a student without it, with on-policy reverse-KL working well and success depending on low teacher-student KL. For the drafter, the natural privileged teacher is the same drafter given the true Qwen3-8B hidden states over the (drafted or ground-truth) block; the student sees only verified-prefix features.

### Cited Findings
- Privileged Information Distillation for Language Models (Penaloza et al., ServiceNow/Mila; arXiv:2602.04942): transfers capabilities learned with training-time privileged information (PI) to policies acting without it. pi-Distill uses one shared-parameter model as both PI-conditioned teacher and unconditioned student trained jointly (weight alpha); OPSD samples from the student and adds a reverse-KL penalty toward the PI-conditioned teacher (on-policy). On Qwen3-8B TravelPlanner, pi-Distill reaches 44.1% vs. 32.3% for SFT-with-CoT + RL (base 23.6%); tau-Bench retail 31.1% vs 29.1%; out-of-domain tau-Bench airline OPSD 14.0% vs 8.0%. Analysis: for pi-Distill, low teacher-student KL and useful PI matter most; for OPSD, PI informativeness predicts success. — [arXiv:2602.04942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04942)
- Draft-OPD's setup is itself a privileged-teacher scheme in the speculative setting: the target model (which has full computation over the drafted prefix) provides per-token distributions on draft-induced prefixes. — [arXiv:2605.29343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29343)
- [BACKGROUND] Learning Using Privileged Information (Vapnik & Vashist, 2009) and Generalized Distillation (Lopez-Paz et al., 2015, arXiv:1511.03643) formalized teacher-with-privileged-features -> student-without. Not re-fetched this session. — [arXiv:1511.03643](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03643)

### Inferences
- Shared-parameter privileged teacher for the drafter (pi-Distill analogue): one drafter network, two modes. Teacher mode: K/V from true Qwen3-8B hidden states at drafted positions (available at training time from target forward passes on the drafted block, or on ground truth). Student mode: K/V from verified prefix only (or from the 0.6B estimates). Train jointly with KL(teacher || student) on block logits. Since parameters are shared, the teacher-student KL stays low, which the pi-Distill analysis identifies as a key success factor.
- Three-tier ladder: privileged teacher (true target states) -> estimator-conditioned drafter (0.6B states, k rounds) -> one-pass student. The middle tier can also be trained with the privileged teacher, improving the estimator loop before collapsing it.
- OPSD analogue: sample blocks from the student drafter, compute teacher-mode logits on the same blocks with true target states, apply reverse KL. This is Draft-OPD with a richer teacher (privileged drafter instead of only target next-token distributions), and can be mixed with Draft-OPD's acceptance-aware loss.
- Risk: if privileged features carry information the student cannot infer from the prefix (e.g., the target's decision on an ambiguous token), the student learns to hedge; mode-seeking reverse KL (OPSD) is the better choice in that case, as also suggested by DMD/T3D results.

### Gaps
- No work found that applies privileged-information distillation specifically to speculative drafters or to diffusion/block drafters.
- The arXiv abstract page for 2602.04942 could not be fetched directly; details come from the arXiv PDF v3.
